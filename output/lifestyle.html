Final Fantasy 7 Revelation Has 3-Person Synergy Attacks But Only in “Emotional or Dramatic Moments”

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Final Fantasy 7 Remake’s combination of real-time combat with pause, harnessing the classic’s ATB in unique ways, resulted in some of the most enjoyable RPG combat out there. It’s hard to believe it could get better, but Rebirth upped the ante significantly, especially with new Synergy Attacks (which first debuted in Episode INTERmission).

By comparison, Final Fantasy 7 Revelation doesn’t add too much to these established systems (not counting FITS, new party members and mounted combat). However, director Naoki Hamaguchi told PlayStation Access that there are three-person Synergy Attacks. If that sounds utterly broken, well, it would be, which is why there’s a catch.

“Talking about this with [battle director Teruki] Endo-san, obviously there are normal abilities. Synchronized abilities, the Limit Breaks, and so we’d be adding to that as well. And if we added in the three-person Synergy Attack, then obviously that’s going to be the strongest one at the top of the tower, and everyone’s going to want to use that, which will make battles monotonous.

“So we decided not to make it something that could be used at any time. Instead, we decided to make it more dependent on the gameplay experience, where there may be a particularly emotional or dramatic moment. So I hope you’ll be looking forward to when they may pop up.”

Making them feel more special makes sense, and Final Fantasy is no stranger to such dramatic moments. Remember the climactic battle against Sephiroth, which involved using a special move (a moment recreated in stunning fashion with Final Fantasy 7 Advent Children’s climax)? Of course, when you start thinking about memorable combat moments with three characters, the possibilities become almost endless.

Final Fantasy 7 Revelation launches on April 7th, 2027, for Xbox Series X/S, PS5, PC, and Nintendo Switch 2. It’s a massive adventure and could easily take over 100 hours to complete, per Hamaguchi, but that’s only the beginning, as multiple playthroughs are apparently required to see everything. And that’s not even getting into the post-launch expansions that focus on Sephiroth and Vincent Valentine.

Species with U‐shaped geographic ranges as an opportunity to study early stages of divergence: The case of Ipomoea microsepala

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →
Abstract Premise The study of the early stages of divergence between populations is crucial to understanding how species and biodiversity originate. Geographic distributions that capture some aspects of the process of divergence, such as those that are markedly U‐shaped, could offer a window to identify systems undergoing incipient speciation, which have traditionally been difficult to identify. Methods We selected Ipomoea microsepala, a species of morning‐glory with a markedly U‐shaped distribution, as a candidate to evaluate potential incipient evolutionary divergence. We used a combination of principal component, phylogenetic, population, and correlation analyses (Mantel tests) of data derived from leaf morphometrics, climatic information, and genome‐wide DNA variation (RADseq) that span the whole species range to assess potential divergence in genomic, climatic, and morphological dimensions. Results We showed that Ipomoea microsepala achieved its current geographic distribution through historical range expansion by successively colonizing adjacent areas in a fashion that is consistent with a stepping‐stone model and isolation by distance. Phylogenetic and population genetic models supported three present leading fronts through which I. microsepala could continue to expand its range and diverge. Plants in these fronts are already genetically distinct, do not exhibit introgression, and use marginally different climate spaces, but lack differences in leaf morphology. Conclusions A geographic perspective continues to be useful to identify and shed light on the process of divergence. Further ecological experimentation with a genomic perspective is necessary to assess the degree to which the differentiation we documented is congruent with early stages of speciation.

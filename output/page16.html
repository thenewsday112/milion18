Anthropic Blocks AI Game Clones in Claude Opus Update

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Anthropic has issued an update for Claude that puts a stop to the AI-generated game clones that have filled platforms like Steam.

The term 'AI slop' is something we've heard a lot in the past few years, but it became especially prevalent in the gaming community after Valve launched Steam Next Fest earlier this month. PC users quickly launched complaints that their feeds had been filled with low-effort clones not only of pre-existing games but even some titles that had only just released their own demos.

As players trudged through the slop, developers faced a reality that threw months or even years of hard work into jeopardy. Some, including Drunken Goddess Reflux developer Alliance Arts and Who You, even took action by canceling their Steam demo plans in favor of private playtests. It's a situation that rapidly spun out of control, prompting a response from Valve itself as it said it would be "thinking about how to solve this problem."

Claude Code picked up a new rule around Oct 27, sent from Anthropic’s servers under a flag called "tengu_heron_brook". There’s no setting to turn it off.

The full rule: “Do build original games, games in a genre, and games from the user’s own designs and assets. Don’t build a recreation of a specific existing game, even if it is renamed or called original.”

As reported by Game Developer, X/Twitter user Chasm was one of the first to share evidence of tech company Anthropic taking action against video game copycats. Based on their findings, Claude, the company's AI assistant, recently received an update featuring a new rule. The full rule can be read below:

"Do build original games, games in a genre, and games from the user’s own designs and assets. Don’t build a recreation of a specific existing game, even if it is renamed or called original."

When reached for comment by Game Developer, Anthropic pointed to a post shared by Addy Osmani, a member of its technical staff. Osmani's explanation can be read below:

"We added two sentences to Claude Code's prompt on Opus 5.5 to steer it away from recreating someone else's specific game because using Claude to infringe IP is against our usage policy. Original games, genre games, and games built from your own designs and assets are meant to keep working. We'll continue to iterate to ensure we're striking the right balance. In the meantime, if Claude turns down something that's yours, send it my way."

Whether there was a specific event or reason that prompted Anthropic to make these changes is unclear.

Game developers and game enjoyers will no doubt find comfort in Anthropic's changes, though it's worth noting that how impactful any of this will be remains to be seen. At the very least, those who use Claude to rip ideas directly from those who used their minds and time to bring them to life should have a tougher time trying to profit off of the work of others.

The industry is struggling to come to grips with artificial intelligence. Some, such as Epic Games' Tim Sweeney, have insisted that Valve should stray from asking developers to disclose games utilizing generative AI materials. Others, including Super Smash Bros. mastermind Masahiro Sakurai, have compared AI-generated games to "low-quality goods." The developers behind VRChat, meanwhile, recently came under fire for how they are handling AI content.

Gears Of War Should Have Killed Off Marcus Fenix Years Ago

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Gears of War is arguably the most masculine property in video games. A franchise founded on the idea of muscular, refrigerator-sized dudes tearing apart stinky underground monsters with badass chainsaws until they’re covered in bloody viscera was always destined to attract such a reputation, but this only makes the moments when it subverts our expectations all the more impactful. Instances of quiet within its post-apocalyptic world, or conversations shared between its main cast when everything seems lost, always hit the hardest. 

Once you strip away the chainsaw bayonets, you’re left with a profound narrative of grief as Marcus Fenix tries to save a humanity that is already flirting with extinction. While all of his muscles and bravado might make the man seem infallible, Fenix is frequently tasked with the impossible--to save a human race that is constantly under the threat of annihilation. He’s doomed to fail, to eventually fall alongside his comrades until a new generation emerges to continue the fight. This makes perfect narrative sense, and would also give the iconic hero some much needed rest. So, what would happen if The Coalition actually went through with it and killed him?

Ever since I first laid eyes on Marcus Fenix as he explored the darkened streets of a fallen city to the melancholic beauty of "Mad World," I have viewed him as a tragic character. He’s fighting an impossible war after being freed from unjustified imprisonment, and spends the entire original trilogy watching close friends succumb to their personal demons or fall to an enemy that shows no signs of slowing. Making the player feel doomed from the outset can be an incredibly powerful storytelling tool, so it’s no surprise that E-Day focuses so heavily on the world falling apart as a younger roster of heroes is forced to take action. 

When you turn up to save the day amidst the rubble of Kalona, it’s often about picking up the pieces while walking over fresh corpses of thousands, where finding even two survivors is a reason to celebrate given the circumstances. Not once does Fenix entertain leaving innocent people behind, and you truly feel for him when retreat becomes the only option. Unfortunately, once the excellent opening act concludes, the campaign settles into a predictable routine that can’t help feeling middle-of-the-road.

This question has been on my mind for over a decade now, since the character arc of Fenix came to a natural conclusion in Gears of War 3 after the Locust are defeated and humanity is guaranteed a future. Our flawed hero might be stricken with grief by the sacrifices of Dom and his other comrades, but he remains surrounded by people who love and care about him in ways that can now be treasured in a world where survival is guaranteed. But thanks to this need to keep popular franchises alive and avoid disappointing legions of fans, Marcus Fenix has remained integral to Gears ever since. 

While the details are cloudy all these years later, there was a time when Gears of War was potentially going to leave the planet of Sera behind in pursuit of pastures new. Speaking to Game Informer as part of a cover story back in 2016, creative director Chuck Osieja claimed that The Coalition was originally thinking about taking Gears to a fresh planet with an all-new cast of characters who were only loosely connected to what came before. So long as Gears focuses on the machinations of conflict with satisfying combat and a worthwhile story, I can see it taking place anywhere, but without the stewardship of Epic Games it settled for the most predictable destination imaginable. Marcus Fenix has a son, the Locust comes back, and humanity once again finds itself on the backfoot. Turns out the original trilogy was for naught. 

Gears of War 4 centered on a new trio of likeable characters--JD Fenix, Kait Diaz, and Del Walker--who were all raised in a world without the Locust threat, but still living beneath the thumb of an outdated militaristic society that refuses to leave old habits behind. It’s a more saturated and freeing world, but the very same darkness awaits beneath the surface. As for Marcus Fenix, he has secluded himself in the wilderness after the death of his wife and the trauma of perpetual conflict, resentful of a son who decided to join a military he has actively come to despise. The war may be over, but Marcus has little interest in returning to a world that refuses to learn from its mistakes. 

This new trilogy would eventually shift focus away from the Fenix family and instead mostly  on Kait and her strange connection to the new Locust threat, with Gears 5 offering a harsh and sudden player choice in the final act as you’re asked to save the life of either JD or Del. Whoever you don’t choose is murdered before your eyes, but since the trilogy was ditched, at least for now, for a prequel in E-Day, we never get to witness the repercussions. Even Marcus seems pretty unfazed by the death of his only son, when this could have been a killer moment for decades of anger, grief, and resentment to culminate in something meaningful. Instead it’s brushed aside and never mentioned again, joining Halo’s Reclaimer trilogy as stories ripe with potential that Xbox left behind because market relevance was more important.

Rather than outliving his son and watching a world he helped save once again fall to ruin, Marcus Fenix should have perished in Gears of War 4 as he passes the torch onto a new generation. Not mauled by a horde of Locust in gruesome fashion as a twisted joke, but giving his life to people he cares about while cementing an enduring hope that things are worth fighting for. Beginning his new character arc as a jaded old man deciding to shield himself from the realities of a world he helped save before reaching a position where he possesses renewed hope in the coming generation would fit the narrative themes while being brave enough to leave our own nostalgia behind. Even in the same universe, we shouldn’t be afraid to fall in love with new characters and new stories, and realise titans like Marcus Fenix are fallible creatures in their own right. Gears of War 4 excelled in making such an iconic symbol of video game masculinity seem vulnerable, but failed to pull the trigger where it mattered most.

Joe Manganiello Says He 'Will Never Give Up Hope' on His D&D Dragonlance TV Series

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Joe Manganiello, one of the most famous Dungeons & Dragons players in Hollywood, said he still wants his Dragonlance TV series to come to fruition despite its cancellation several years ago.

The Justice League and One Piece actor was hired as showrunner and screenwriter on the show years ago, promising a live-action adaptation of one of the stories that helped introduce him to tabletop role-playing. After a series of snags, though, Manganiello eventually went on to confirm that his live-action Dragonlance would no longer come to be.

IGN asked Manganiello about his ill-fated D&D project during a conversation at Lucca Comics and Games 2026. He walked us through how, after five years of work, things fell apart, while also teasing that the door for a Dragonlance TV show – and even his documentary – will never close.

"When both of those got put on the shelf due to a change of leadership – due to shuffling going on within [Entertainment One] – those projects became nobody's child," he explained. "I mean, I was hired by Michael Lombardo, who was my old boss at HBO. He hired me to create a television show, to create the next Game of Thrones for him, but then he was gone.

"He's my guy. He's the guy who understood my pitch. He's the one who believed in me. He's the one who saw what it could be, and he's gone. And then, my other producing partner, he was gone, too. So, the two people that I was there with are gone, and the people there had different ideas. All of a sudden, those things become the priority as I'm turning in my script. Now, my people aren't there. Same with the documentary."

Manganiello went on to say that, as his hands-on time in the D&D universe began to fall apart, he had "nobody helping to fight with" him to bring then-in-development projects to life. With Dragonlance officially confirmed to be canned in 2024, it was around this time the actor thought "OK, I'm out," but as he told us, "there is new hope."

"[President of Wizards of the Coast & Hasbro Digital Games] Dan Ayoub is a fantastic leader. He gets it, and we work really well together," he continued. "I think there are talented people within the building now that are doing some amazing things, and we're going to do some amazing things together.

"The dream was always, always to be off running a Dragonlance series, and I will never give up hope on that. And I think there's a lot of people that share that vision. I think the documentary with Danny Ayoub is actually going to be closer to getting out as well."

That's two big updates on two D&D projects that many fans believed were destined to never see the light of day. Still, as the pieces move into place on the board behind the scenes, it's best not to get hopes up too high until Manganiello and the rest of his team have more to announce. For example, Ayoub took up his position as president just four days ago after previously serving as senior vice president of Dungeons & Dragons. It's unclear how this title change may have affected any plans Manganiello mentioned during our interview.

The world of Dungeons & Dragons is trying to come to the live-action world in more ways than one. After a movie adaptation, Honor Among Thieves, launched to critical success and lackluster commercial response in 2023, we've still yet to see a sequel. There is allegedly a second script floating around somewhere, but whether it will leap off the page remains to be seen. Decido comunque di sfidare le leggi del suddetto Dio e ordino.

La tecnica, almeno all’apparenza, è quella dei supplì moderni, con una panatura dalla grana grossolana, che non ho mai amato particolarmente. Nella maggior parte dei casi risulta eccessivamente dura (che non necessariamente significa croccante) e finisce per rubare la scena al ripieno. E invece no. Alla pizzeria Unica a Milano la panatura è croccante e ben fritta, ma resta al suo posto. Lasciando spazio a un riso cotto alla perfezione, condito con un pomodoro al basilico strepitoso e un ripieno generoso di fiordilatte filante.

Ne avrei ordinato volentieri un altro, ma capirete più avanti che ho fatto bene a fermarmi.

Le pizze di Unica, pizzeria romana a Milano

Ordiniamo due pizze. La Prima è una Margherita. Ordino sempre la margherita quando provo una pizzeria per la prima volta. Mi permette di non farmi fregare dalle comparse, rischiando di perdere di vista il protagonista.

Alla vista è già bella e croccante. I bordi leggermente bruciacchiati mi fanno sentire subito a Centocelle.

A distanza di quasi venti ore riesco ancora a ricordare la sensazione del primo assaggio. È lei, è proprio la mia pizza romana. L’impasto è soffice e poi croccante, e lo definirei persino luminoso. Perché il bordo, rigido ma comunque alveolato, ha una superficie quasi lucente.

Lei è la pizza scrocchiarella romana. Le imitazioni si smascherano subito, le riproduzioni sono quasi sempre fallimentari.

Dopo averne mangiata metà, mi avvento anche su quella del mio compagno di merende, una Margherita con bufala e Parmigiano Reggiano 36 mesi. Non credevo che potessero esserci differenze così marcate, ma signori miei, gli ingredienti comandano. Anche quelli che sembrano avere un ruolo secondario. La bufala dà una spinta diversa, mentre il parmigiano è lì per restare e per urlare che sulla pizza, effettivamente, ce deve sta’!

Rispetto alla mia, forse risulta un po’ più asciutta, ma immagino che il pomodoro abbia lasciato spazio alla bufala. Anche perché lei quello spazio se lo sarebbe preso comunque, e senza pietà!

La Pepperoni che ora è di moda

Un criminale (così definito dopo aver ucciso i miei buoni propositi nel giro di due secondi), seduto accanto a noi, ha ordinato la mia pizza preferita, la pepperoni. Faccio quindi una cosa che non avevo mai fatto prima: ordino una terza pizza. Proprio quella.

Qui troviamo pomodoro alla ’nduja, salamino, fiordilatte e pecorino.

L’aspetto è commovente. Il salamino, piccolo, sottile, rosso e malefico, è bruciacchiato ai bordi e tutto arricciato. Incazzato (si può dire?).

Incurante della notte che potrebbe aspettarmi, afferro con voracità la prima fetta. Penso che, se l’avessi scelta come prima pizza, l’avrei sbranata in due secondi, senza lasciare spazio ad alcuno spirito critico riconoscibile.

È una pizza ricca di sfumature e, anche se non amo la ’nduja insieme al salamino sulla pizza (Dio dei miei natali calabresi, perdonami), devo ammettere che qui tutto funziona. Sia chiaro: amo la ’nduja e amo, ovviamente, il salamino, ma preferisco tenerli separati. Eppure, in questo caso, si incontrano senza sopraffarsi. In bocca, la cremosità del pomodoro e del fiordilatte accompagna la croccantezza del salamino, dando vita a una sinfonia di giubilo!

Ero stremata, eppure quell’ultimo pezzo, che mi spettava di diritto, mi guardava come se fosse già orfano e disprezzato. E allora ho concluso il mio dovere, terminando una cena che poteva trasformarsi nell’ennesima delusione e che invece è stata una liberazione. Dalla concorrenza, dal Dio del Botox dei supplì e dalla distanza da Roma mia.

Unica dista esattamente 650 metri da casa mia. Vedete voi quale Dio ringraziare per me, adesso.

Il ritorno a Milano di Jacopo Mercuro che apre Unica Pizzeria Romana: l’assaggio

31 ottobre 2026 – di Emanuele Bonati

Jacopo Mercuro è tornato a Milano con Unica Pizzeria Romana. Aperta in via Morosini, al confine tra Porta Vittoria e Porta – appunto – Romana.

Sempre di pizza romana si tratta ma diversa da quella di Pizzottella, in via Muratori, ora chiusa e precursore dell’ondata di ristorazione in partenza dalla Capitale. Allora pizza al taglio in teglie rettangolari. Oggi, Jacopo Mercuro porta a Milano con Unica la sua pizza tonda alla romana. Il nome scelto indica la sua unicità e diversità rispetto alla pizza in teglia di 180Grammi, cioè i suoi locali a Roma.

Magari non tanto “unica” considerato che Jacopo Mercuro arriva dopo diverse aperture romanesche a Milano. E soprattutto dopo un concorrente temibile. Cioè Luca Pezzetta che ha aperto la pizzeria Futura giusto un anno fa e che ha aperto la sua seconda Futura a Milano, in porta Genova. Guarda tu lo stesso 30 ottobre di Unica di Jacopo Mercuro.

Mercuro, 39 anni, e Pezzetta, 38 anni, sono due veri e propri fenomeni del mondo della pizza romana, già acclamati e carichi di riconoscimenti. Saranno loro i futuri e unici Gabriele Bonci – che di anni ne ha 49 – dei prossimi anni? Lo scopriremo.

Premessa: con il menu della sua pizzeria Unica, Mercuro vuole portare a Milano un percorso costruito intorno alla croccantezza. Si parte con i fritti, sia quelli della tradizione che quelli più innovativi (Unici). E si prosegue con le pizze, in un menù che propone pizze Margherite, Old School e Uniche.

1 pizza e 2 fritti

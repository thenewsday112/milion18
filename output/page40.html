You don’t want to get bogged down in an overload of data and recommendations. You have goals and you want the most streamlined way to reach them.

Of the many best workout apps for beginners, MyFitnessPal is one of the easiest to use thanks to its simple, familiar layout and AI-supported tools that can help take the guesswork out of everyday diet decisions. Below, see how it compares with nine of the best fitness apps for beginners in 2026.

How We’re Defining “Beginner-Friendly” Fitness Apps

What’s the best  workout app for beginners that’s the easiest to get started with and keep you motivated? Here are the main criteria we’re using:

  • Simple onboarding: Getting started should require just a few quick steps to understand your goals and lifestyle and get you oriented to the app’s main features.
  • Clear, uncluttered layout: The best workout app for beginners should offer a clear interface that provides only the information you need to support your daily goals.
  • Intuitive food logging: To get the most out of a diet and fitness app, consistently logging your food is important. The best workout app for beginners should provide easy and accurate logging capabilities.
  • Low daily time commitment: Logging, tracking, and planning on the app shouldn’t take up so much of your time and mental energy. Those should be reserved for creating healthy meals and building good habits.
  • Helpful guidance, but not too much. The best workout app for beginners should offer helpful tips and personalized recommendations, but not overload you with too much data and information.
  • AI or automated features that reduce decision fatigue. AI-supported tools can help take the stress out of daily tasks, such as macro scheduling and meal planning.

With each of these points in mind, MyFitnessPal consistently ranks the highest among the best fitness apps for beginners. With an intuitive layout, the app is easy to use from day one. 

With MFP, you can customize your goals, start logging your food with the AI-supported Meal Scan and Voice Logging tools, and figure out what to eat using Meal Planner. All these features are accessible within minutes of signing up. 

The Best User-Friendly Fitness Apps For Beginners In 2026

MyFitnessPal: Best overall fitness app for beginners 

Of the best workout apps for beginners, MyFitnessPal tops our list as #1 overall. It offers simple, AI-supported fitness and nutrition tracking for beginners who want an easy way to start building healthy habits. 

Never tracked your meals before? No problem. These features make it easy:

  • Massive food database: Find nearly everything you eat in the extensive restaurant and food database of over 20 million foods.
  • Barcode scanner: Easily add packaged foods to your food diary with a scan of the barcode.
  • Meal Scan: Instantly snap a quick pic to log your meal.
  • Voice Logging: Skip the typing and simply say what you ate.
  • Saved meals: Quickly log the food and meals you eat regularly.

A free account covers the food database, logging and saved meals. Barcode scanning, Meal Scan and Voice Logging come with Premium, and Meal Planner with Premium+.

Along with intuitive food logging, MyFitnessPal helps take the stress and guesswork out of what to eat with Meal Planner. This AI-supported feature creates a custom plan with recipes that align with your calorie and macro targets, dislikes, allergies, and diet preferences. 

Each day, you can easily follow your goals and check your progress over longer periods of time. As you grow more comfortable with the app, you can dig deeper into more advanced features, such as in-depth nutritional analyses, macro balancing, and customized Workout Routines, available with Premium. 

MyFitnessPal is easy on day one and will become a powerful habit by day 100. It’s one of the best workout apps for beginners because it scales with your goals  whether your focus is weight loss, strength-building, or maintenance. MFP is also flexible to changes in your schedule and lifestyle.

Lose It!: Suited for simple, no-frills calorie tracking

Lose It! offers simple calorie tracking and goal setting with a main focus on weight loss. The app’s clean interface makes it easy for beginners to get started. It calculates your daily calorie budget, a number determined by metrics such as your current weight, average activity level, and the amount of pounds you aim to lose in a specific time period. 

Among the best workout apps for beginners, Lose It!’s simplicity can be a drawback, however. You need to manually log your food and exercise, which can be more time-consuming versus MyFitnessPal’s automated logging tools. 

To get access to more advanced features, including a barcode scanner, macronutrient tracker, and food and exercise planner, you need to upgrade to Lose It! Premium for $79.99 a year. There’s no monthly option.

Both the free and paid versions are limited in recipe and meal plan recommendations.

BetterMe: Suited for guided, follow-along workouts

BetterMe Well-Being Coach is a fitness-oriented resource that includes a catalog of guided workouts, many of which can be done at home and with little or no equipment. For beginners, the app offers structured exercise classes and workout plans that range from Pilates to calisthenics to strength training with dumbbells. 

While the app can be helpful for daily exercise ideas and movement goals, nutrition is less of a focus. BetterMe includes a simple calorie tracker and some recipes and diet plans, but is more structured within multi-week programs instead of long-term goals. 

To access BetterMe’s workouts, you need to pay for one of its subscription tiers, which vary in price. For example, one 4-week plan came out to $38.95 in January 2026. 

Among the best workout apps for beginners, BetterMe doesn’t publish prices and quotes a figure at the end of its onboarding quiz, so what you’re offered may differ. You can also join fitness challenges or work 1:1 with a BetterMe coach, each for an additional cost.

Yazio: Suited for beginner-friendly recipes

Yazio offers an uncluttered experience for simple calorie counting and beginner-friendly recipes. The free version of one of the best workout apps for beginners includes basic tracking tools and a barcode scanner. However, if you’re looking for help in the kitchen, you have to sign up for YAZIO PRO. 

Sold as a three-month or twelve-month plan charged upfront, there’s no monthly option, and prices appear at checkout. The PRO version includes a database of recipes that can be filtered by preparation difficulty, diet preferences (such as sugar-free or low-carb), and ingredients. 

The PRO version also lets you set up intermittent fasting trackers, which block out certain meals and snacks during specified time periods. To track movement, you have to either manually input your steps or connect the app to a third-party fitness tracker, such as Apple Health or Health Connect.

While YAZIO PRO’s recipe catalog offers some meal inspiration, the amount of options can be overwhelming. PRO also adds Future Meal Planning, which lets you log meals in advance.

Lifesum: Suited for aesthetics and meal plans 

Relative to the other workout apps for beginners, Lifesum emphasizes simplicity with its sleek layout and basic food and exercise tracker. 

The app highlights a weekly Life Score based on 16 nutrition and exercise measures, though you need Premium to see the full detail behind it. This can look like your fruit and vegetable intake, hydration levels, and exercise variety. 

If you’re a beginner looking for meal ideas, you’ll need to sign up for Lifesum Premium at $14.99 a month, $29.99 for three months, or $99.99 a year. This subscription unlocks a selection of structured meal plans and programs for losing, maintaining, or gaining weight. 

For example, you can follow a Paleo plan for 21 days that includes recipes and a shopping list. 

However, the meal plans are not customizable and not all of them support food preferences or allergies. With no room for flexibility or personalization, these structured plans can be difficult and frustrating to follow, especially as you’re easing into a new diet.

Noom: Suited for beginners who want mindset lessons

Noom is a weight-loss tool that emphasizes the psychology behind your diet, lifestyle, and relationship with food. The app is based around a color-coded food tracker that ranks food from most nutrient-dense (“green”) to most calorie-dense (“orange”). 

While this can take the stress out of calorie counting, it doesn’t allow you to dive deeper into nutritional data that can be helpful for making healthy decisions on your own. 

Noom also provides bite-sized daily lessons grounded in psychology and behaviour change, but doesn’t offer direct support for your unique health goals. Noom Weight costs $209 a year, or $70 a month. 

Meal planning and AI coaching sit on a separate Premium add-on, billed on top, whose price Noom doesn’t publish. Aside from the ability to add your steps with a third-party tracker, Noom lacks fitness-oriented features compared to other workout apps for beginners.

WeightWatchers (WW): Suited for community-style support 

WeightWatchers (WW) is an established brand for weight-loss support, known for its proprietary Points system. Similar to Noom’s color-coded tracker, the focus is not on counting calories but choosing food based on nutritional quality. 

This limits the potential for more precise nutrition tracking as you transition out of the beginner stage. Also, fitness is not a central feature within the WW program.

WW also stresses the importance of a support system. The basic Core program, which costs $24 monthly, gets you access to the member community, while the Core+ program at $44 monthly, includes unlimited coaching and in-person and virtual workshops. 

WW’s structured program is not as flexible or customizable as the other best fitness apps for beginners on this list like MyFitnessPal. This makes it potentially less sustainable for long-term use.

MyNetDiary: Suited for macro-curious beginners

MyNetDiary is heavy on data, with a strong focus on calorie, macro, and micronutrient tracking. Compared to the other best fitness apps for beginners, this one can track up to 108 nutrients, which can be an overwhelming amount of information, especially when you’re just starting out. 

However, if you’re a beginner wondering how to balance your daily macros, MyNetDiary offers macronutrient targets that align with your goals, such as losing fat or building muscle.

For $59.99 annually, you can upgrade to a Premium subscription to get access to “Advanced AutoPilot,” which automatically adjusts your calorie and nutrient targets based on your progress. You can also manually map out your meals for the week with the Premium tier’s Meal Planner, but this can be time-consuming without automated tools. For AI-powered features, AI Coach, meal suggestions, a restaurant menu scanner, and voice logging are now available inside MyNetDiary itself on a Premium Plus tier, added in January 2026. 

Yuka: Suited as a beginner-friendly scanner companion 

Yuka’s main feature is its barcode scanner, which analyzes the health impact of food and cosmetic products. The app rates products from “excellent” to “bad” and provides more specific information, like if a product is “a bit too caloric” or “contains additives to avoid.” 

Yuka Premium uses open pricing: an annual sliding scale starting at $10 in the US, with identical features whichever amount you choose. It unlocks offline scanning, an advanced search that finds a product’s grade without scanning it, dietary-preference alerts, and unlimited scan history.

As far as the best workout apps for beginners go, Yuka is easy to use and can be helpful at the grocery store. This is especially true if you’re starting a new diet plan or searching for products that better align with your nutrition goals. However, it’s not a comprehensive nutrition and fitness tool like MyFitnessPal.

Cal AI & Cronometer: Suited only if you love AI or deep data

Cal AI is an emerging AI-native tool, while Cronometer goes deep on data tracking. Both are some of the more niche workout apps for beginners this list covers. 

Cal AI is beginner-friendly especially if you enjoy fast AI logging with nutritional data. The app includes voice logging and photo scanning, but it doesn’t publish a database size. 

Cal AI has no free tier, though it offers a three-day free trial. Its published in-app purchases range from $2.99 to $29.99.

Cronometer uses charts that track up to 95 nutrients and a “Macro Scheduler” that’s available with a Gold subscription at $10.99 monthly. However, Cronometer has no recipe database or ready-made meal plans, though Gold can schedule foods to appear in your diary automatically. 

Gold now also includes voice logging and Crono Coach, an in-app assistant.  Both tools are relevant for beginners who want fast logging.

How To Pick The Right Beginner-Friendly Fitness App

If your aim is to lose weight, build muscle, or simply start building healthier habits, here are some questions to consider when deciding on the best workout app for beginners:

  • Do you prefer structure or flexibility? Are you looking for a structured plan with lots of guidance or an app that allows for changing goals and schedules?
  • Do you want deep data or bigger-picture info? Are you overwhelmed by numbers and charts? Do you prefer to focus on just a few metrics as you track your progress?
  • Do you value speed above all else? Are you short on time and looking for an app that can help you log and plan your meals quickly?
  • Do you want an app to use for the long term? Are you looking for a tool that adapts to your progress and offers advanced features that you can grow into?

As a beginner, MyFitnessPal is the best all-around starting point. Its automated tools, simple tracking, and robust set of features can help support your short-term goals and long-term health.

For most beginners, MyFitnessPal is the best overall user-friendly app because it combines intuitive design, easy logging, and AI support that helps you feel confident in making healthier decisions from the get-go. 

The rest of the best fitness apps for beginners specialize in just one or two user-friendly features, such as BetterMe’s guided workout plans, Yazio’s easy recipe options, and Noom and WeightWatchers’ structured tracking systems. However, they lack the depth and breadth of MyFitnessPal when it comes to tracking your food, movement, and progress, and unloading the stress of everyday decisions, like “What do I eat?”

FAQs about beginner-friendly fitness apps

What is the best fitness app for total beginners in 2026?

If you have specific health goals for 2026 but feel intimidated by nutrition and fitness tracking, MyFitnessPal is the strongest all-around choice among the best fitness apps for beginners. After signing up and setting your goals, you can start logging your food with the AI-supported Meal Scan and Voice Logging, planning your meals with Meal Planner, and learning more about building healthy habits for the long term.

Is MyFitnessPal easy enough for someone who has never tracked before?

Absolutely. MyFitnessPal makes tracking easy from the start, and the free tier covers everything you need to begin: the food database, food and exercise logging, saved meals and custom goals. With its simple, intuitive design, you can easily track your food and movement. For an even more seamless experience, you can upgrade to a Premium tier to get access to super-fast logging tools including Meal Scan, which lets you snap a quick pic to log your meal.

Do I need to understand macros to use MyFitnessPal?

To start, all you need to know is that macros are short for “macronutrients,” which include carbohydrates, fats, and proteins. Upon signing up, the default macro percentages are 20% of calories from protein, 50% from carbohydrates, and 30% from fat. However, you can customize your macros using the Macro Calculator, which will adjust these percentages based on metrics like your body weight and your goals.

What Are The Best Health Apps In 2026?

MyFitnessPal Premium: Best overall value

While the free tier provides only a weight chart, the upgraded version includes more data related to your nutrient and calorie intake, diet trends, and body measurement changes.

MyNetDiary Premium is available for $59.99 a year, with a higher Premium Plus tier at $99.99 a year that adds AI logging and coaching tools, and also comes with a feature called “Advanced AutoPilot,” which adjusts your calorie targets based on your progress.

The Gold subscription also includes a recipe importer that allows you to get nutritional data for your custom recipes, but doesn’t offer a recipe database or ready-made meal plans, though Gold can schedule foods and recipes to appear in your diary automatically. 

Fitness is less of a focus on Cronometer, but you can sync the app with devices such as Fitbit and Polar.

Cronometer Gold’s value is in its advanced macro and micronutrient data, which can be seen in intricate charts and timelines. While such depth can provide helpful insights, it can be overwhelming and typically not needed for building healthier habits.

BetterMe is free to download with in-app purchases, though it doesn’t publish what the free version includes. You can pay more for fitness challenges or to work with a BetterMe coach. 

For $99, you can also purchase a BetterMe wearable Fitness Tracker to sync with the app and track your steps, heart rate, and sleep. BetterMe’s value compared to the other best apps for fitness is in providing a range of guided workouts, but is limited in offering support for specific health and diet goals.

WeightWatchers (WW): Value for coaching + structured programs

When you walk through the Brazilian Cerrado and look down at the ground, you might find a myriad of flowers blooming within a single square metre. Take five more steps, and that diversity may have completely changed. Two weeks later, in that very same spot, the earlier blooms may well have vanished to make way for entirely new species. In the Cerrado, plant life does not unfold at an even pace. It is a dynamic dance orchestrated by two grand conductors: time and fire.

A study published in Annals of Botany showed how the taxonomic, functional, and phylogenetic diversity of bee-pollinated plants and their interactions with bees are structured across time and space, analysing data from 1,200 small 1 m² plots over two years. The aim was to determine what drives greater change in floral display and bee visits: the physical distance between patches or the passing of weeks? And what happens when a fire sweeps through this mosaic?

The Brazilian Cerrado. Photo by Helena Lopes from Pexels.

Distance did not explain the differences between the flowers. Two neighbouring square metres could be just as different from one another as two points hundreds of metres apart. The true conductor of change is time. The Cerrado's rapid phenological succession means that the composition of flowering plant species shifts continuously every few weeks, ensuring a steady, ever-renewing supply of nectar and pollen for pollinators.

Yet when a fire struck part of the study area, the researchers were able to compare the exact “before and after”. The fire broke dormancy and stimulated the flowering of several adapted species, increasing the likelihood of finding blooms scattered across the terrain. However, there was a decrease in the overall abundance of flowers, owing to bud damage on woody species such as trees and shrubs. Even so, the diversity of floral shapes and evolutionary lineages remained stable, ensuring that varied types of resources continued to be available for bees. The bees responded swiftly to this altered landscape. Although the number of visits per flower dropped temporarily, the bees began foraging across the terrain in a much broader, more evenly distributed manner.

Fire across the Cerrado landscape. Photo by Arthur de Magalhães Goulart (Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0).

Furthermore, fire reduced the abundance of tree flowers, curtailed the dominance of the honeybee (Apis mellifera), creating space and opportunities for diverse solitary and social native bees to visit Cerrado flowers, giving native species a fighting chance.

Conserving the Cerrado calls for an understanding of the harmony within this vast ecological symphony. It is a duet between time and fire where every movement matters: the coexistence of burned and unburned patches, the rapid turnover of flowers week by week, and the many other factors explored across ecological research together compose the arrangement that keeps the dance of native pollinators fine-tuned and resilient, conducting the continuous entrance of new floral instruments.

This Chicken and Broccoli Skillet Lasagna is creamy, cheesy, and packed with tender chicken, broccoli, and soft lasagna noodles, all made in one pan! The Easy Way to Make a Creamy Chicken Lasagna

Some nights, boiling pasta and tossing it with a jar of sauce is all I have the energy for. But when I’m craving something a little more special without spending ages layering a lasagna, this is my happy middle ground. And my favorite budget-friendly part? I only need 1 lb. of chicken breast (usually just 1-2 breasts!) to make six hearty servings, thanks to the noodles and broccoli bulking everything out. I season and sear the chicken first, then sauté the onion and garlic in the same skillet. The broken lasagna noodles cook right in the chicken broth, soaking up all the flavor from the garlic and Italian seasoning as they soften. It’s a similar idea to our skillet lasagna, but with a creamy chicken and broccoli twist!

To keep this a true one-pot meal, I don’t cook the broccoli separately, either. I chop it finely, stems and all (because why waste them?!), and add it towards the end so it stays tender and bright green. Then a little heavy cream and Parmesan bring everything together in a rich, comforting sauce, and the mozzarella on top gives you a classic cheesy finish. If you’re in the mood for a more traditional, oven-baked version, our white chicken lasagna is another delicious option to try next.

Jennie Alley making her chicken and broccoli skillet lasagna in our Nashville test kitchen.

Recipe Success Tips

  1. Break the noodles into roughly 2-inch pieces. I find this size works well for stirring the lasagna noodles through the skillet without them getting tangled up. It makes scooping out individual servings much easier, too.
  2. Give everything enough room to cook. You’ll need a large, deep, broiler-safe skillet with a lid, about 12 inches in diameter. I recommend a heavy-bottomed skillet because it heats more evenly, helping the noodles cook at the same rate without sticking to the bottom (see the skillet I use in the photo above).
  3. Keep the lid on during simmering. Press the broken noodles down so they’re mostly submerged in the broth, then cover and maintain a gentle simmer. I give them an occasional stir to separate any pieces sticking together, then pop the lid straight back on. The trapped steam helps cook the noodles that aren’t completely covered by liquid.
  4. Leave a little liquid in the skillet! The noodles will keep absorbing moisture as the sauce comes together, so the mixture should still look slightly loose once the pasta and broccoli are tender. If the noodles are still firm but most of the broth has disappeared, add a splash of water or broth, cover, and let them cook a little longer. Nobody wants dry chicken and broccoli lasagna!
  5. Be gentle with the cream and cheese. I turn the heat down to low before stirring in the heavy cream, Parmesan, and shredded chicken. Boiling the sauce can make it separate or turn grainy. Also, keep a close eye on the mozzarella under the broiler. It only needs 2-4 minutes to turn bubbly and lightly golden.
  6. Bulk it up with more veggies! I’m all for using what I’ve got, and I think frozen peas, frozen corn, or drained canned corn would be delicious here! Add them with the broccoli so they have time to heat through. For firmer veggies like carrots, dice them small and sauté them with the onion so they have longer to soften. A couple handfuls of fresh or frozen spinach would also be lovely, like I use in my vegetarian stovetop lasagna. Just stir it in near the end until wilted.

Chicken and Broccoli Skillet Lasagna

This creamy Chicken and Broccoli Skillet Lasagna recipe makes 6 servings in just 50 minutes! Tender chicken, broccoli, and cheesy noodles, all made in one pan. Instructions

  • Gather all ingredients.
  • Stir together salt, black pepper, paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, and Italian seasoning in a small bowl. Slice chicken breast in half and season thoroughly with half of seasoning blend.
  • Finely chop the broccoli, including the stems, dice the onion, and mince the garlic.
  • Heat olive oil in a large, deep oven-safe skillet medium-high heat. Sear chicken 4-5 minutes per side until fully cooked through (165℉ internally). Remove and set aside.
  • Reduce heat to medium and In the same skillet, add onion and garlic and sauté for 3-4 minutes until softened.
  • Add chicken broth and broken noodles. Stir and press noodles down so they’re mostly submerged. Cover and simmer on medium-low for 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  • Stir in the broccoli and reserved seasoning mixture. Cover and continue cooking for 4-5 minutes, or until the noodles are tender and the broccoli is bright green. The mixture should still look slightly loose. If needed, add a splash of water to loosen it.
  • While the noodles finish cooking, shred the chicken into bite-sized pieces.
  • Reduce the heat to low and stir in the heavy cream, Parmesan cheese, and shredded chicken until well combined and creamy. Be careful not to allow the mixture to boil to avoid curdling.
  • Sprinkle the mozzarella evenly over the top. Transfer the skillet to the oven and broil for 2-4 minutes, or until the cheese is melted, bubbly, and lightly browned.
  • Remove from the oven and allow to cool for 3-5 minutes before serving. Enjoy!

Mix the seasonings and prepare the chicken: In a small bowl, combine 1¼ tsp salt, 1 tsp black pepper, 1 tsp paprika, 1 tsp garlic powder, 1 tsp onion powder, and 1 tsp Italian seasoning. Slice 1 lb. chicken breasts in half horizontally to create thinner cutlets. Season both sides with half the spice mixture, saving the rest for later.

Prep the vegetables: Finely chop 2 broccoli crowns (270g, about 3 cups), including the tender stems, dice 1 small yellow onion (140g, about 1 cup), and mince 3 cloves garlic. Keep the broccoli pieces small so they cook quickly and mix evenly with the noodles.

Sear the chicken: Heat 1 Tbsp oil in a large, deep, oven-safe skillet over medium-high heat. Add the seasoned chicken and cook for about 4-5 minutes per side, until lightly golden and the thickest part reaches an internal temperature of 165°F. Transfer the chicken to a plate and set aside. Leave those flavorful browned bits in the skillet.

Sauté the onion and garlic: Reduce the heat to medium and add the diced onion and minced garlic to the same skillet. Cook for 3-4 minutes, stirring frequently, until the onion softens and becomes translucent and the garlic smells fragrant. Stir across the bottom of the skillet to loosen the browned bits left behind by the chicken.

Add the noodles and broth: Pour in 4 cups chicken broth and add 8 oz. broken lasagna noodles (about 9-10 sheets, broken into roughly 2-inch pieces). Stir to distribute the noodles, pressing them down so they’re mostly covered by the broth. Don’t worry if a few pieces stick out!

Cover the skillet, reduce the heat to medium-low, and simmer gently for 8-10 minutes. Stir occasionally to separate any pieces sticking together, then replace the lid.

Add the broccoli and remaining seasoning: Stir in the 3 cups chopped broccoli and the reserved seasoning mixture. Cover and cook for another 4-5 minutes, or until the noodles are tender and the broccoli is bright green. The mixture should still look slightly loose and saucy. If the noodles need more cooking time but the liquid is running low, add a splash of water or broth.

Shred the chicken: As the noodles finish cooking, use two forks to pull the cooked chicken into bite-sized shreds. I like keeping the pieces fairly small so there’s plenty of chicken throughout the finished skillet.

Make it creamy! Turn the heat down to low and stir in ¾ cup heavy cream, ½ cup grated Parmesan, and the shredded chicken. Gently mix until everything is coated in a creamy sauce and the chicken is heated through. Avoid letting the mixture boil vigorously, as this can affect the texture of the cream sauce.

Add the mozzarella and broil: Sprinkle 1 cup shredded mozzarella evenly over the skillet. Transfer it to the oven and broil for 2-4 minutes, until the cheese is melted, bubbling, and lightly golden brown in spots. Keep an eye on it so the cheese doesn’t burn!

Rest and serve: Carefully remove the skillet from the oven and let the lasagna sit for 3-5 minutes. The sauce will thicken slightly as it rests, leaving you with tender noodles, shredded chicken, and broccoli in a creamy, cheesy sauce. Scoop into bowls and enjoy!

What to Serve with Chicken and Broccoli Skillet Lasagna

I like serving this pasta warm, straight from the skillet, with a little extra Parmesan sprinkled over the top. A squeeze of fresh lemon juice or a little lemon zest just before serving also brightens the creamy sauce, and a pinch of red pepper flakes adds a little heat if desired. This creamy chicken broccoli lasagna skillet makes 6 hearty servings of about 1½ cups each, so if I want anything extra, I find a small side is plenty enough.

I like serving this dish with a fresh salad and a lemony vinaigrette to balance out the creamy sauce. Our Mediterranean coleslaw and green bean salad are both delicious options, and you can prep either one ahead of time! I’d just go with whichever you already have the ingredients for to keep things easy and budget-friendly. A slice of garlic bread is also a no-brainer! I like making a batch of our homemade freezer garlic bread, then pulling out a few slices whenever I need them. Toasted sourdough is another easy option, or, if you’ve planned ahead, our no-knead focaccia is lovely for mopping up any leftovers.

Storage & Reheating

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3-4 days. Let it cool slightly before transferring it to your container, and refrigerate within 2 hours of cooking. The noodles will absorb the sauce as they sit, so expect leftovers to be a little thicker.

Reheating

I recommend reheating individual portions gently in the microwave or in a skillet over low heat. Add a splash of milk or chicken broth to loosen the sauce and bring back that creamy consistency. Stir occasionally until heated through to 165°F, being careful not to overheat the sauce.

Freezing

This recipe is best enjoyed fresh, but you can freeze leftovers if needed! Let the chicken and broccoli lasagna cool, then transfer it to airtight, freezer-safe containers and freeze for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating gently in the microwave or on the stovetop. I recommend adding a splash of milk or broth to help restore the creaminess, as the sauce may separate slightly after freezing and the noodles will be softer.

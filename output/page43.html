Personalised cancer vaccine expected to be approved next year

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
A scanning electron micrograph of a melanoma cell Science Photo Library/Alamy

A personalised mRNA vaccine for melanoma, the deadliest form of skin cancer, is expected to be approved for use in the US, Europe and the UK next year. The vaccine is designed to stimulate immune responses against genetic mutations that are unique to an individual’s cancer. When used alongside standard treatment in a late-stage trial, it substantially cut the risk of cancer recurrence or death within the next two years.

“I think approval could be quick, and next year sounds reasonable,” says Alan Melcher at the Institute of Cancer Research in London, who wasn’t involved in the trial.

“It is a huge deal,” says Rebecca Lee at the University of Manchester, UK, who also wasn’t involved. Thousands of people in the UK alone could benefit from this, based on the trial’s patient population, she says. “It’s highly likely the US and Europe [including the UK] will approve it next year.” However, producing the vaccine is very expensive, and it may not be deemed cost-effective for use on the UK’s National Health Services (NHS), she says.

In August, drug-maker Moderna announced that the vaccine had positive results in a late-stage trial. More specific findings have now been released.

The trial recruited more than 1100 adults who had surgery to remove high-risk melanomas that were likely to recur or spread to distant sites. In the UK, 19,400 melanoma cases are diagnosed annually, of which about 25 per cent are deemed high risk.

Two-thirds of the participants were randomly assigned to receive a bespoke vaccine on top of standard immunotherapy, called pembrolizumab, which helps the immune system find and destroy cancer. Both were delivered every three weeks for up to a year. The remaining participants received placebo injections plus pembrolizumab.

The vaccine is made by genetically sequencing a small sample of a person’s cancer and comparing that sequence with the DNA in the individual’s healthy blood cells. This enables researchers to pinpoint mutations specific to the cancer.

Based on the same technology used in covid-19 vaccines, the cancer vaccine contains mRNA molecules that encode for proteins with up to 34 of those mutations. “The jab contains a fingerprint of proteins specifically associated with the cancer,” says Lee. “This helps [immune] T-cells recognise the cancer better, and then pembrolizumab prevents those T-cells from becoming exhausted once they’ve started attacking the cancer cells.”

About two years after the start of treatment, 82 per cent of the participants who received the vaccine plus pembrolizumab were alive without their cancer recurring, on average, compared with 70 per cent among those on pembrolizumab alone. “It’s a really exciting result,” says Lee. Side effects were similar between the two groups.

The results combine cancer recurrence and death rates into one figure, but since a treated cancer has to recur for it to cause death, this combined measure should be interpreted mostly as a sign of the vaccine reducing recurrence, says Melcher. The participants haven’t been tracked for long enough to robustly determine whether the vaccine improves survival rates. These results should be available within a few years, says Lee.

A revolutionary way of targeting tumours could beat even the worst cancers 

We have long treated cancer based on its location in the body. But a whole new approach that analyses tumours differently is now helping people survive forms of cancer that were once considered a death sentence 

But because the vaccine has to be tailored to and manufactured for each individual, it is expected to be very costly, which could affect NHS access. “If it was a cheap drug, I’d definitely say it will get approved [for NHS use], but it’s going to be expensive,” says Lee. In January, a report by Reuters suggested it would cost around $200,000 per treatment. Moderna and Merck (also known as MSD), which was also involved in the vaccine’s development, did not respond to a request about its cost.

Further research is needed to better understand who is mostly likely to benefit from the vaccine, says Melcher. Some people may have mutations included in their vaccine that fail to stimulate anti-cancer immunity as predicted, or pembrolizumab could work so well that the vaccine can’t lead to further improvement. “If we can find immune markers in the blood or tumour that predict patient responses, we could use those to determine who to give the jab to,” says Melcher.

James Larkin, also at the Institute of Cancer Research, says personalised vaccines could also work for other types of cancer, such as those affecting the lungs, bladder and kidneys, which are also routinely treated with immunotherapy. Further research is needed to verify this and explore whether the vaccines could work as standalone treatments.

This Stuffed Pepper Soup has all the cozy flavors of classic stuffed peppers, with browned, savory ground beef, tender bell peppers, onions, and garlic simmered in a rich, generously seasoned tomato broth. I honestly could not stop eating it! It’s easy, hearty, satisfying, and you get all that familiar flavor without the extra steps of stuffing peppers. I serve it over fluffy rice and, don’t worry, I didn’t forget the cheese! A sprinkle of mozzarella gives you that irresistible cheese pull. Better yet, it’s ready in just 45 minutes and costs only $1.97 per serving. Perfect for soup season, or any season!

Big Flavor from a Simple Shopping List

I already love classic stuffed bell peppers, but this soup makes enjoying those same flavors so much easier on a weeknight. In most stuffed pepper recipes, the rice goes straight into the filling. For this soup, though, I prefer to keep it separate until serving. Rice soaks up liquid like a sponge, and I found that stirring it straight into the pot made the rice mushy. And by the next day, there was barely any broth left! Instead, I like to spoon the rice into individual bowls and ladle the soup over the top. It’s such a simple step, but it makes a big difference…especially when I’m saving leftovers.

I also make the most of my cooking time by getting the rice going while I prepare the soup. Once the ground beef and veggies (bell peppers, onion, and garlic) are cooked, I simmer everything for 15 minutes in a rich tomato broth packed with herbs. And, of course, I wanted to keep this soup easy on the grocery budget, too. I use green bell peppers (which are usually cheaper than red, yellow, or orange), canned tomatoes, dried herbs, and Better Than Bouillon for the broth. Nothing fancy or expensive, but plenty of flavor! These simple ingredients help me stretch just one pound of ground beef into six hearty servings. 😋

Recipe Success Tips

  1. Let those peppers stand out! I cut my bell peppers and onion into a medium dice because I like them to have some texture and really stand out in the finished soup. Keep the pieces roughly the same size so they cook evenly. After the sauté and 15-minute simmer, the peppers should be tender enough to pierce with a fork but still hold their shape. If they’re a little firm, give the soup a few more minutes.
  2. Remove the excess grease. Depending on the type of ground beef you use (I use 27% fat), quite a bit of grease can collect in the pot as it cooks. Make sure to carefully blot up the excess with a paper towel so your soup doesn’t end up oily. You can also spoon the grease into a heatproof container, let it cool, and discard it in the trash. Just don’t pour it down the sink!
  3. Make it as brothy as you like. Prefer a slightly thinner soup? Stir in a splash of extra beef broth or water until you’re happy with the consistency. If there’s more liquid than you’d like, let it simmer uncovered for a few extra minutes, stirring occasionally. I recommend tasting and adjusting the seasoning before serving.
  4. Got extra veggies? Throw them in! This soup is a great way to use up whatever you’ve got. Diced carrots or celery can go in with the peppers and onion, although carrots may need a little longer to soften. Corn can be added with the tomatoes and broth, and fresh spinach only needs a minute or two at the end. You can even add diced potatoes (I’d dice them small, like in our vegetable beef soup), but they’ll need a longer simmer and possibly some extra broth!

Budget-Saving Tip

When I make stuffed peppers at home, I typically use ground beef. However, the ground beef makes up about half the cost of this entire pot of soup, so it’s also a great place to save some money. Feel free to use ground chicken, turkey, pork, or sausage instead. (You can even try lentils!)

Higher-fat ground beef is often cheaper than lean, and it’s totally fine to use here. Just drain off the excess fat after browning so your soup doesn’t end up oily. If you still want some beef in your soup but need to stretch your budget further, try ½ lb. ground beef with 1 cup cooked brown or green lentils. Brown the beef as directed, then stir in the cooked lentils with the tomatoes and broth to heat through during the final simmer. We use a similar meat-stretching trick in our lentil and sausage stew, and it’s a great way to make a little meat go further.

how to make Stuffed Pepper Soup step-by-step photos

Gather all of your ingredients: Cut 2 green bell peppers into a medium dice, finely dice 1 onion, and mince 2 garlic cloves. I like to get the chopping done first since the rice and soup cook at the same time.

Cook the rice: Add 1 cup uncooked long-grain white rice and 2 cups water to a medium saucepan with a lid. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat to a gentle simmer. Cover and cook for 20 minutes, or until the rice is tender and the water has been absorbed. Turn off the heat and set aside. (This is the same technique we use in our ‘how to cook rice’ guide).

Brown the ground beef: While the rice simmers, heat 1 Tbsp vegetable oil in a large pot over medium heat. Once the oil is hot, add 1 lb. ground beef and cook for about 5 minutes, breaking it into small crumbles as it cooks. The beef should be cooked through, with no pink remaining.

Remove the excess grease: Carefully blot up any excess grease from the beef using paper towels. This helps prevent an oily layer from forming on top of the finished soup.

Soften the vegetables: Add the 2 diced green bell peppers, 1 diced onion, 2 minced garlic cloves, 1½ tsp salt, and 1 tsp black pepper to the pot with the beef. Cook for 5 minutes, stirring occasionally. The onions and peppers should start to soften but still have some texture.

Add the tomatoes and seasonings: Pour in one 14.5 oz. can of diced tomatoes (including the juices), one 15 oz. can of tomato sauce, and 2 cups beef broth. Add 1 tsp dried oregano, ½ tsp dried basil, ¼ tsp dried parsley, and ¼ tsp paprika. Stir everything together until the seasonings are evenly distributed throughout the tomato broth.

Simmer the soup: Bring the mixture to a gentle simmer and cook for 15 minutes to allow the flavors to develop and the broth to thicken slightly. The bell peppers should be tender enough to pierce easily with a fork while still holding their shape. If they’re still too firm, let the soup simmer a little longer.

Serve with rice and cheese: Fluff the cooked rice with a fork and divide it evenly among six serving bowls (about ½ cup per bowl).

Ladle a heaping cup of stuffed pepper soup over each portion of rice, then sprinkle with the shredded mozzarella (1 cup total). Serve warm and enjoy! (For a bonus, I sprinkled fresh parsley on top. Most fresh herbs would be a great garnish, like basil, thyme, or chives, or you can enjoy the recipe as is!)

Storage & Reheating

I recommend storing any leftover rice and soup separately because the rice will continue absorbing the broth as it sits. Let everything cool promptly, then transfer to separate airtight containers and refrigerate for up to 3-4 days.

Reheating

Reheat the soup in a saucepan over medium heat, stirring occasionally, or in the microwave until heated through. Warm the rice separately in the microwave (with a splash of water, stirring every 15-30 seconds until hot), then spoon the soup over the top. If you’ve already mixed the rice into the soup and it’s become too thick, add a splash of beef broth or water to thin it out as it reheats.

Freezing

You can freeze leftover stuffed pepper soup for up to 3 months in freezer-safe containers. I recommend freezing the rice separately to keep it from getting mushy. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator, or use the thaw setting on your microwave, then reheat thoroughly before serving.

Work vehicles rarely leave the dealer finished. Fleets add ladder racks, toolboxes, utility bodies and light bars. In dry or fire country, a growing number of owners add one more item: a water tank with a pump.

That upfit is now available in an electric form. Skid units from makers such as BLZ Fire Skids drop into a utility task vehicle (UTV) bed or pickup box. A 24-volt battery runs the pump, and the host vehicle can recharge it while running. The result is a vehicle story as much as a fire story.

Payload, range, mounting and handling all change once several hundred pounds of water ride in the back.

What Does a Full Water Tank Do to a Vehicle’s Ratings?

Two numbers on the door-jamb sticker matter most. Gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) is the most the loaded vehicle may weigh. Payload is what you can add on top of the empty vehicle, people included.

Water is the heavy part of any skid. Each gallon you add puts about 8.3 pounds in the bed. BLZ lists its Ranger 60 at roughly 300 pounds dry. Fill its 60-gallon baffled tank and you add close to 500 pounds more. That puts the working load near 800 pounds before anyone climbs in.

A simple way to check the math on your own rig:

  1. Read the sticker. Note the payload figure for your exact trim.
  2. Subtract the crew. Count every rider at a realistic weight.
  3. Subtract the wet skid. Use dry weight plus water, never dry weight alone.
  4. Check what is left. Tools, hose and fuel cans all draw from the remainder.

EV pickups make this step more important, not less. Their battery packs are heavy, so payload varies widely between models and trims. Never assume a figure from a brochure. The sticker on the truck in front of you is the one that counts.

Which Platform Suits a Water Upfit?

The right vehicle depends on where the fire is likely to be. BLZ sizes its range across three broad platforms:

  • Compact UTV. The 45-gallon Scout is built for UTVs, small trucks or trailers. It suits tight trails and fence lines.
  • Full-size UTV or half-ton pickup. BLZ says fire departments have mounted the Ranger 60 in chief trucks and F-150 beds. Burn crews run them in Can-Am side-by-sides.
  • Flatbed or utility body. The Defender 200 is a slide-in unit with a 200-gallon tank, foam capability and up to 1,200 psi.

A UTV reaches places a pickup cannot. A pickup covers road miles faster and carries more. Many rural fleets end up running one of each.

Why Pair an Electric Pump With the Host Vehicle?

Portable fire pumps have long used small gas engines. California’s Air Resources Board classes small off-road engines as spark-ignition units of 25 horsepower or less. Those engines face tighter rules every few years.

A gas pump also adds chores that fleet managers know well:

  • A second fuel to buy, store and rotate before it goes stale.
  • A pull start that may balk on a cold morning.
  • Exhaust and noise right beside the operator.

BLZ’s electric design removes the separate pump engine. Its Redline pump delivers up to 16 gallons per minute (GPM, the flow rate) and up to 175 psi on the Ranger 60. The onboard battery runs it for up to four hours on its own.

The vehicle link is the clever part. BLZ’s Fight-Finity system replenishes the skid battery from the host UTV while the engine runs. The company describes the result as effectively unlimited runtime. EV owners will recognize the idea as a managed power share.

How Do Electric UTVs and EV Pickups Change the Plan?

Battery-electric work vehicles raise a fair question. Can the whole rig run without fuel?

Big fleets have already tested the idea. Several cities have trialed electric fire trucks with hybrid range extenders. They keep a fuel backup because a fire call cannot wait for a charger.

Smaller rigs face the same limits. Heavy loads, steep grades and summer heat all cut EV range. A full water tank brings all three at once. Plan range for the loaded trip out and the trip back.

But, there is an upside. Some electric pickups can act as mobile power stations for tools and battery chargers. Utilities sometimes cut grid power during high fire danger. A truck with exportable power can keep other batteries topped up when the shop has none.

Will the Truck Corner and Brake Differently?

Yes, and drivers feel it on the first slope. Water shifts as you turn and brake. Internal baffles slow that surge, which is why BLZ builds them into its tanks.

Height matters as well. The Ranger 60 stands about 37 inches tall at the hose reel. Every inch of mass above the bed floor raises the center of gravity. EV engineers place battery packs low for the same reason.

A few habits help the rig stay planted:

  • Use the factory tie-down cleats or through-bolts, not plastic trim.
  • Keep the heavy end close to the cab and centered side to side.
  • Slow down on side slopes, especially with a half-full tank.

How Can a Work Vehicle Avoid Starting a Fire?

A clean drivetrain still runs hot underneath. Smokey Bear’s vehicle fire prevention advice notes that a hot exhaust or undercarriage can ignite dry grass. Loose safety chains and bare wheel rims can also throw sparks onto the roadside.

Electric drive removes the exhaust pipe, but brakes and bearings still heat up. Stick to established tracks in cured grass. Park on gravel or bare dirt, and check chains before every tow.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Payload Should I Budget for a Ranger 60?

BLZ lists the Ranger 60 at about 300 pounds dry. Water adds close to 500 pounds when full. Plan for roughly 800 pounds against your payload.

Will It Fit in a Half-Ton Truck Bed?

Many can, depending on trim and payload. BLZ notes Ranger 60 units mounted in F-150 beds. Check the door-jamb sticker before you buy.

Does the Skid Battery Drain the Vehicle?

The skid carries its own battery for the pump. BLZ’s charging system feeds that battery from the host while it runs. Confirm the wiring with both makers first.

Is a Water Upfit a Substitute for 911?

No. A skid helps owners and crews reach small fires sooner, but call 911 first whenever a fire starts or spreads.

Au Muséotrain à Semur-en-Vallon, dans la Sarthe, le 25 octobre prochain, les visiteurs doivent s’attendre à frissonner (gentiment) de peur : ils croiseront des monstres, des fantômes et autres spectres présents pour fêter Halloween.

Le thème choisi cette année ? « Le Muséotrain hanté », annonce Christian Pottier, président de la Compagnie du Chemin de fer de Semur-en-Vallon. « Pour cette édition 2026, nous proposons deux nouveautés spécialement destinées aux enfants.

Les petits doivent dénicher des mini-fantômes dissimulés un peu partout dans le musée pour gagner les fameux bonbons d’Halloween. Les plus grands sont invités à chercher quatre lettres cachées dans le décor pour trouver le fantôme qui hante le musée… »

Dimanche 25 octobre de 14h30 à 17h (horaires habituels des trains). Rens. : Muséotrain de Semur, La Gare, 72390 Semur-en-Vallon. Tél. : 06 30 84 41 33. ccfsv333@gmail.com

Scientists propose a way to detect dangerous “mirror life” before it exists

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →
Scientists have proposed a way to detect “mirror life,” a hypothetical form of life that could pose serious risks to humans, animals, and ecosystems if artificially created. Using special fluorescent molecules and a type of polarized light also used in 3D movie technology, researchers believe they could distinguish ordinary organisms from their mirror-image counterparts.

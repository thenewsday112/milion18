Let’s face it: McDonald’s is everywhere. Whether you’re making a quick Micky D’s run to fuel up for a busy day or your body is craving the McNuggets you fell in love with as a kid, it’s normal to dine now and then at one of the most recognizable fast food chains on the planet.

The good news is that you’ll find some options at McDonald’s that will let you enjoy your favorite fast food restaurant without derailing your nutrition and weight loss progress. We’ve rounded up the 9 healthy McDonald’s food orders from breakfast through dinner so you always know what to reach for.

Is There Healthy Food at McDonald’s?

Yes, you can find healthier options at McDonald’s. It’s possible by choosing lower-calorie items and smaller portions. (1)

Fast food is often high in sodium, calories, and fat. Too many of these things can affect both your mental and physical health. That’s why moderation is key. If you crave those iconic fries or a classic burger, it’s okay to indulge once in a while. Making smart choices and simple swaps at fast food joints like McDonald’s means you can honor your cravings in a way that feels good for your mind and body. (2, 3, 4)

Here are some handy things to remember the next time you order at McDonald’s:

  • Whether you choose chicken or beef, the protein content will be similar but from a fat and calorie standpoint, the chicken sandwiches aren’t always the lighter choice, so make sure to check the nutrition info. (1)
  • It can be tempting to supersize your meal, but sticking to smaller portions keeps your wellness journey order on track and will help you feel good about your choices later.
  • When scanning for healthier McDonald’s options, aim for items under 400 calories; you’ll find more than a few on the menu below. (1)

The Healthiest Food at McDonald’s: 9 Dietitian-Approved Picks

Katherine Basbaum is a registered dietitian who works with MyFitnessPal. We asked her what she looks for when considering the healthiest McDonald’s items.

“The healthiest options, in my view, tend to include the menu items that aren’t loaded with a ton of sauces and cheese as well as those with options for reduced serving sizes (4-piece instead of 10) and a lower calorie count (400ish and below),” Basbaum explains. (20)

Keeping that in mind, some of these picks are the healthiest food at McDonald’s that follow Basbaum’s guidelines. We’ve covered you for breakfast, lunch, and dinner so that you can enjoy McDonald’s anytime!

1. Fruit and Maple Oatmeal

Snag healthy McDonald’s food for breakfast with a bowl of fruit and maple oatmeal. Oats have tons of health benefits since they’re high in antioxidants, are a source of whole-grain fiber, and can help lower cholesterol. 

“When I think of healthy food at McDonald’s, their Fruit & Maple Oatmeal is at the top of my list. The main ingredients are fresh, whole foods that come together to create a warm, filling breakfast under 400 calories. The rolled oats provide fiber and complex carbohydrates, natural sweetness comes from the fresh and dried fruit, and there’s just a touch of light cream and brown sugar for a bit of richness,” Basbaum says. 

For these reasons, oatmeal is considered one of the healthiest breakfast items to help you start your day. (5) This oatmeal has 320 calories, 4.5 grams of fat, and 6 grams of protein, making it an all-around healthy choice. (6)

2. Egg McMuffin

If you like a savory fast-food breakfast, the original Egg McMuffin is one of the top McDonald’s healthier options to consider. It has 310 calories, 13 grams of fat, and 17 grams of protein. 

This could be a better option if you’re counting macros, as it has 30 grams of carbs compared to oatmeal, which has 64. (6,7)

Thanks to the egg, cheese, and lean Canadian bacon, it also has a nice protein boost. In fact, at 17 grams of protein for just 310 calories, the Egg McMuffin is one of the highest-protein breakfast options at McDonald’s.(1,7)

3. 4-piece Chicken McNuggets With Small Fries

McDonald’s chicken nuggets are iconic. If McNuggets are your jam, go for the 4-piece size, which has just 170 calories, 10 grams of fat, and 9 grams of protein, making it one of the lowest-calorie healthier options at McDonald’s on the menu. “Chicken options like the McNuggets,” notes Basbaum, “generally contain much less saturated fat than the options that go heavy on the beef and cheese.” (1,8)

And thanks to the low-calorie count, you can pair your nuggets with a side of small fries and still keep your meal under 500 calories. An order of small fries has 230 calories and 11 grams of fat, not including dipping sauces like ketchup. (8,9)

4. Filet-O-Fish Sandwich

The Filet-O-Fish is a McDonald’s classic with 380 calories, 19 grams of fat, and 16 grams of protein. (10)

Even though it’s better to eat broiled or seared to save on fat, fish has several health benefits and is usually quite lean. You can ask for it without cheese or sauce to lower the fat content or enjoy it as is. (11,12)

5. Classic Hamburger: The Healthiest Burger at McDonald’s

When thinking about the lowest-calorie food at McDonald’s, you might be surprised to discover that the classic burger has just 250 calories. With the included toppings, it has nine grams of fat and 12 grams of protein, which isn’t too bad. It’s widely considered the healthiest burger at McDonald’s (1,13,15)

As with the nuggets, you can pair a burger with small fries and still come in under 500 calories. (9,13)

6. Apple Slices

A side of apple slices is the healthiest food at McDonald’s, so feel free to pair it with anything else on the menu. One serving has just 15 calories, zero fat, and just 4 grams of carbs. When considering sides, it’s a much healthier choice than fries or other sweet treats. (1,9,14)

7. Hash browns

Perfectly golden and crispy, hash browns are hard to resist when stopping by McDonald’s in the morning for breakfast. Each has 140 calories, 8 grams of fat, and 2 grams of protein. A hash brown alone might not fill you up, so pair it with any of the other breakfast items mentioned on this list and still enjoy an entire meal for under 500 calories. (16)

8. Sausage Burrito

If you’re craving something a little different for breakfast, try the sausage burrito, which has 310 calories, 17 grams of fat, and 13 grams of protein. It’s a nice alternative to a breakfast sandwich filled with eggs and sausage for protein and peppers and onions for a bit of veggies. You’ll also find some melty cheese inside, which always makes everything taste better! (17)

9. 3-Piece McCrispy Strips

If you’re looking for a satisfying lunch or dinner option with a good amount of protein, McCrispy Strips deserve a spot on your radar. With 350 calories, 17 grams of fat, and a whopping 30 grams of protein, a 3-piece order is one of the best high-protein healthier options at McDonald’s under 400 calories. (1,18)

McDonald’s describes McCrispy Strips as crispy, juicy, peppery chicken strips made with all white meat chicken and coated with golden-brown breading and black pepper flavor. (18)

“Chicken options generally contain a lower ratio of saturated fat than the options that go heavy on the beef and cheese,” Basbaum reminds us and the 3-piece McCrispy Strips are a prime example. Just be mindful of dipping sauces, which can add extra calories depending on what you choose. (1)

Make Healthy Eating at McDonald’s a Habit

The bottom line is that you can fit in the occasional stop at  McDonald’s when you’re trying to make healthier choices. As Basbaum reminds us, the trick is to stick with McDonald’s higher-protein lower-calorie items and practice mindfulness and moderation to enjoy what you’re eating in the moment and not overthink things. 

“But for the ultimate in wholesome ingredients, balanced nutrition and mindful preparation,” Basbaum says, “there’s simply no place like home.” (8,13,14,18)

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Can you eat healthy food at McDonald’s?

You can make healthier choices at McDonald’s that fit into a balanced diet, depending on your nutritional needs and preferences. The classic hamburger and 4-piece Chicken McNuggets are two options that can be paired with a side to create a meal. Rather than focusing on calories alone, consider the overall nutritional value of your meal, including protein, fiber, sodium, and other nutrients, as well as portion sizes. (1,8,13)

What is the lowest calorie thing at McDonald’s?

Apple slices are the single lowest-calorie item at just 15 calories, but if you’re looking for a complete meal, the classic hamburger (250 calories, 12g protein) or the 3-piece McCrispy Strips (350 cal, 30g protein) are among the most balanced options on the menu. Pair either with apple slices instead of fries to keep the meal well under 500 calories. (1,13,14,18)

Does McDonald’s have healthy breakfast choices?

Though perhaps not as healthy as the light and nutritious breakfast you’d make at home, McDonald’s breakfast includes some genuinely balanced choices. The Egg McMuffin (310 calories, 17g protein) and the Fruit and Maple Oatmeal (320 calories, 6g protein, whole-grain fiber) stand out as the best picks. The key is skipping the oversized combo meals and high-sodium sausage platters and sticking to single items under 400 calories. (1,6,7)

What is the healthiest burger at McDonald’s?

The classic hamburger is the healthiest burger at McDonald’s, with just 250 calories, 9g of fat, and 12g of protein, which is the lowest calorie count of any burger on the menu. If you want a bit more protein, the McDouble adds a second patty for around 400 calories and 22g of protein, still a reasonable choice by fast food standards. (1,13,15,19)

100 years of Mail Rail to be celebrated with programme of events

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The Postal Museum will celebrate 100 years of Mail Rail next year with a new exhibition.

Credit: The Postal Museum

The London-based museum, which opened in 2017, will launch Powering the Post, it’s new exhibition focused on stories of invention, engineer and experimentation behind the post, including lessons learned from the ideas that didn’t deliver, in Mail Rail’s centenary year.

In 1927, the General Post Office opened the world’s first driverless, electric railway, now known as Mail Rail. At its peak, the service carried four million letters a day beneath London’s streets.

The network would go on to connect eight subterranean stations, from Paddington to Whitechapel.

The railway closed in 2003, but The Postal Museum allows visitors to ride through the network’s underground tunnels.

The museum has announced plans to celebrate 100 years of Mail Rail with a programme of events, lead by the new temporary exhibition, opening on 29 May 2027.

The exhibition will focus on stories of invention and experimentation behind the post – including lessons to be learned from the ideas that didn’t deliver, like Rocket Mail, a failed attempt at delivering letters by missile.

Co-produced with postal workers and local residents from communities local to Mail Rail’s Mount Pleasant station, the exhibition will feature interactive elements which curators hope will inspire the next generation of problem solvers.

Chris Taft, Head of Collections, The Postal Museum, said, “In 1927, an underground fleet of driverless electric trains provided a pioneering solution to a city whose streets were quickly filling with traffic. In Mail Rail’s centenary year, Powering the Post will celebrate the inventive engineers whose spirit of experimentation has helped keep us all connected.”

Further details of the Mail Rail 100 programme will be announced in 2027.

I love pork chops, and I feel like they’re under appreciated. Some people think pork chops are dry and boring. Let me tell you, I completely disagree! These Smothered Pork Chops are a Southern staple, packed with flavor and seared to perfection. And the best part? The whole thing comes together in one skillet in just 35 minutes, with simple ingredients and a homemade onion gravy that’s good enough to eat by the spoonful. Grab some mashed potatoes because you’re going to want every last drop of that gravy!

A Good Sear Makes an Even Better Gravy

Everyone has their own way of making Southern smothered pork chops, and this is mine! My secret is seasoning, dredging, and searing the pork chops over medium-high heat first. The light flour coating helps create a delicious, slightly crisp golden crust, and all those tasty browned bits left behind in the skillet add even more flavor to the homemade gravy. I always set the chops aside while I make the gravy, too. Those lean cuts can overcook quickly, and nobody wants tough pork chops for dinner!

Then, don’t even get me started on the gravy. I make it from scratch with onions and garlic (the backbone of any good sauce, if you ask me! 😉). I give the onions time to soften and start browning, bringing out their natural sweetness and adding another layer of flavor to the sauce. A little flour helps thicken everything up, then chicken broth (I use Better Than Bouillon to save money) and a splash of heavy cream give the gravy a rich, luxurious finish. Comfort on a plate without spending a fortune! If you’re a gravy lover like me, you’ll also want to try our Salisbury steak next. It’s another budget-friendly favorite with plenty of homemade gravy.

Melissa Nolan serving her smothered pork chops in our Nashville test kitchen.

Recipe Success Tips

  1. Keep those pork chops juicy! I use boneless pork chops in this recipe, which are fairly lean, so they can dry out quickly. I recommend using an instant-read thermometer to check the thickest part of each chop, especially if yours are thicker than the ones I’ve used. Pork is safely cooked at 145°F, followed by a 3-minute rest. Set the chops aside while you make the gravy, then return them to the skillet briefly to warm through so they don’t overcook.
  2. Give the pork chops room to brown. Let the chops sit at room temperature for 10-15 minutes to help take the chill off. Then, pat them dry with paper towels before seasoning and dredging, and make sure the skillet is hot before adding them. If your chops don’t fit comfortably in one layer, cook them in batches! Crowding the pan traps moisture, making it harder to develop that lovely golden crust. Watch the heat, too, as the flour coating can burn if the pan gets too hot.
  3. Get the gravy consistency right. The butter and reserved flour form a roux, a mixture of roughly equal parts fat and flour that thickens the gravy. Cook the flour for about a minute to get rid of its raw taste. Then stir in the chicken broth, scraping up those flavorful browned bits from the pan as you go. Let the gravy simmer until it’s thick enough to coat the back of a spoon. Too thick? Add a splash more broth. Too thin? Let it simmer a little longer before returning the pork.
  4. Taste before adding more salt. I season my pork with salt, pepper, and paprika, but garlic powder or onion powder are great additions, too! Remember that chicken broths can vary a lot in saltiness. I always taste the gravy after it has thickened before deciding whether it needs more seasoning.
  5. Add some extras! I kept my recipe simple with onions and garlic, but sliced mushrooms are a delicious addition, as I use in my creamy pork chops recipe. Cook them with the onions until the excess moisture evaporates. You can even add bacon!

Smothered Pork Chops

This easy Smothered Pork Chops recipe is a Southern favorite! Juicy, golden-seared pork chops in creamy onion gravy, ready in just 35 minutes. Instructions

  • Gather and prepare all ingredients.
  • Heat the oil in a medium cast-iron over medium-high heat. Pat the pork chops dry with paper towels and season evenly with ¼ tsp salt, ¼ tsp pepper, and paprika.
  • Sprinkle the flour on a plate and dredge the pork chops in the flour. Reserve the remaining flour to make the roux.
  • Once the pan is hot, gently place the pork in the cast-iron. Let the pork cook undisturbed for 3-4 minutes on each side until a golden-brown crust forms. Remove the cooked pork from the pan and set them aside on a plate while you make the sauce.
  • Turn the heat to medium, and add the onions, stirring occasionally. Cook until they start to brown, for about 5 minutes.
  • Melt the butter and add the garlic. Cook for 1 minute.
  • Sprinkle in 2 Tbsp of the reserved flour and cook for 1 minute.
  • Add the chicken broth, heavy cream, thyme, and remaining salt and pepper. Cook until it thickens, for about 5 minutes.
  • Return the pork and any juices to the skillet to warm it through.

how to make Smothered Pork Chops step-by-step photos

Gather and prepare your ingredients. Let the pork chops sit out at room temperature for 10-15 minutes to take the chill off. Slice 1 large yellow onion (about 2 cups) and mince 2 garlic cloves (about 1 Tbsp). Measure out the remaining ingredients so everything is ready to go.

Season the pork chops: Heat 1 Tbsp vegetable oil in a large cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat. Meanwhile, pat 4 boneless center-cut pork chops dry with paper towels. Season both sides evenly with ¼ tsp salt, ¼ tsp black pepper, and ½ tsp paprika.

Dredge the pork chops: Spread ¼ cup all-purpose flour on a plate. Lightly coat each pork chop on both sides, shaking off any excess flour. Reserve the remaining flour to help thicken the gravy later.

Sear the pork chops: Once the oil is hot, carefully add the chops to the skillet in a single layer. Cook undisturbed for 3-4 minutes per side, until a golden-brown crust develops. Avoid moving them too much so the coating can brown nicely. Check that the thickest part of each chop reaches an internal temperature of 145°F. Transfer the chops to a plate and set aside while you make the gravy.

Sauté the onion: Reduce the heat to medium and add the sliced yellow onion to the same skillet. Cook for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onion softens and turns golden around the edges.

Add the butter and garlic: Stir in 2 Tbsp butter and the minced 2 cloves of garlic. Cook for about 1 minute, stirring frequently, until the butter has melted and the garlic smells fragrant. Be careful not to let the garlic burn, as it can make the gravy taste bitter.

Make the roux: Sprinkle 2 Tbsp of the reserved flour over the onion mixture. Stir continuously for about 1 minute, until the flour is fully incorporated.

Make the creamy onion gravy: Add 1 cup chicken broth, ¼ cup heavy cream, ¼ tsp dried thyme, and the remaining ½ tsp salt and ¼ tsp black pepper. Stir well, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom of the skillet. Let the gravy simmer for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it’s smooth and thick enough to coat the back of a spoon.

Smother the pork chops! Return the pork chops and any juices collected on the plate to the skillet. Spoon the creamy onion gravy over the top and heat briefly until warmed through. Enjoy!

What to Serve with Smothered Pork Chops

All this creamy onion gravy deserves something delicious to soak it up! My first choice is a big scoop of mashed potatoes. They’re fluffy, buttery, and perfect for catching every last spoonful of gravy. I’d start the potatoes before cooking the pork chops, since they take about 25 minutes to prepare. For an even cheaper option, serve the chops over white rice. It takes about 25 minutes, too, and can simmer away with the lid on while you work on the pork. Or, for a Southern-inspired dinner, try our cheese grits. They’re cheesy and ready in just 15 minutes, so you can get them going on another burner once you’ve seared the chops.

To round out the meal, I recommend some sautéed green beans. Their garlicky flavor and tender-crisp texture are lovely alongside the creamy gravy, and they add some extra veggies to your plate. They only take about 10 minutes to cook once prepped, so you can sauté them in a separate skillet while the onions and gravy come together. That way, everything is ready to serve at roughly the same time.

Storage & Reheating

Store leftover smothered pork chops in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. I recommend keeping the chops covered with plenty of gravy to help prevent them from drying out. Refrigerate within 2 hours of cooking.

Reheating

I prefer reheating these pork chops in a covered skillet over medium-low heat to help keep them tender. The gravy will thicken as it cools, so add a splash of chicken broth or milk to loosen it up. Heat the gravy thoroughly and warm the pork chops until they reach an internal temperature of 165°F. Try not to heat them any longer than necessary, as boneless pork chops can become tough when reheated.

Freezing

I wouldn’t freeze these smothered pork chops. The heavy cream in the gravy can separate or turn grainy after thawing, and the pork chops may lose some tenderness.

East Lancashire Railway puts lifesaving equipment on the move

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The East Lancashire Railway (ELR) has taken another important step towards enhancing emergency preparedness across the railway with the arrival of a new portable Automated External Defibrillator (AED), secured through fundraising with the support of national defibrillator charity AEDdonate.

The new defibrillator will ultimately be deployed as a mobile unit travelling onboard ELR trains, meaning potentially lifesaving equipment can travel with passengers, staff and volunteers along the railway rather than being restricted to a fixed location.

Before entering operational service, the ELR will put in place appropriate governance, asset management, checking, training and deployment arrangements to ensure the equipment can be managed safely and consistently as it moves between services.

The timing of the new equipment is particularly appropriate, arriving during Restart a Heart this October, the annual initiative led by Resuscitation Council UK to improve cardiac arrest awareness and CPR education and help more people become confident to act in an emergency.

Restart a Heart activity takes place throughout October, with Restart a Heart Day on 16 October. Resuscitation Council UK reports that fewer than one in ten people survive an out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in the UK.

The new AED has been secured following fundraising led by ELR Fund Raising Lead Adrian Dewey, working with AEDdonate.

Mike Kelly, Chairman of the ELR said:

“Keeping staff, volunteers and our visitors safe is our top priority. I want to thank AEDdonate for their timely donation and all of our kind donors for making this possible. In addition to defibrillators located on stations, having this piece of lifesaving equipment available on trains should the worst happen will further enhance our health and safety framework.

The ELR is now looking to build upon the first unit by securing additional mobile AEDs, with the longer-term ambition of increasing coverage across all our operating services.

As a registered charity, the East Lancashire Railway relies heavily upon fundraising, donations, volunteers and the support of businesses and organisations.

Together through this and future campaigns we will continue to make the ELR a safer and more accessible place so we can all enjoy its history and magic.”

Phillip Warren, ELR Commercial Services Director, AED sponsor said:

“This is about putting lifesaving equipment where our passengers, volunteers and staff actually are.

“A railway presents a slightly unusual challenge. Our passengers are not necessarily sitting next to a fixed building or piece of equipment – they may be travelling on a train somewhere along the line. Having a defibrillator capable of travelling with that service gives us another important tool should we ever face a medical emergency onboard.

“It is particularly fitting to be announcing this during Restart a Heart this October. Hopefully this equipment will never be needed, but if that day ever comes, we want to have done everything reasonably possible to be prepared.”

Phillip added:

“Individuals wishing to support the next phase of the mobile AED project can donate through the ELR’s fundraising campaign:

https://www.justgiving.com/campaign/elr-defibrilator-fundraiser

If a business would like to sponsor an AED, or a medical supplier or other organisation is in a position to donate one, we’d be delighted to talk to them. It is a very tangible way of supporting a charity and potentially helping to save a life.”

Chicken Congee

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, chopped fine
  • 2 shallots, chopped fine
  • 3.5oz (100g) boneless, skinless cooked chicken breast, minced 
  • 1/2 tsp low-sodium soy sauce 
  • 2 cups (500g) water
  • 1/2 tsp low-sodium chicken bouillon powder

Can a one-bowl meal really satisfy? This one will. It starts with a base of nutty farro and tender sweet potato, topped with chicken breast and finished with a tangy, herbal chimichurri sauce.

Farro is a great grain option because it contains a good amount of protein and fiber (1). You can also add any roasted vegetable — like broccoli or squash — to this meal to up the fiber content.

Active time: 45 minutes | Total time: 1 hour

Chicken and Sweet Potato Farro Bowl

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (208g) semi-pearled farro
  • 1 medium (130g) sweet potato, cubed (and peeled if desired)
  • 1 lb (450g) boneless, skinless chicken breast
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, divided
  • 2 tsp chili powder
  • 1 1/2 cups (36g) fresh parsley, lightly packed
  • 1 clove garlic, chopped
  • 1/4 tsp crushed red pepper flakes
  • 3 tbsp red wine vinegar

Directions

Preheat the oven to 400ºF (200ºC).

Put 3 cups of water in a medium saucepan and bring to a boil. Add the farro and sweet potato cubes and return to a boil, then cover and reduce the heat to low to simmer for 20 minutes. The farro should be tender. Drain in a wire mesh strainer and let cool. The mixture can be stored in the refrigerator, tightly covered, for up to a week.

Line a sheet pan with parchment, then place the chicken on the pan. Drizzle with 1 tsp olive oil and massage to coat, then sprinkle with the chili powder and rub to coat. Bake for 15–20 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through and an instant-read thermometer reads 165°F (74°C) when inserted in the thickest part of the breast. Cool on a rack until safe to handle. Shred into pieces and reserve.

For the chimichurri, place the parsley and garlic in the bowl of a food processor. Process to mince finely, scraping down the sides to make a fine paste. Add the crushed red pepper, red wine vinegar and remaining 2 tsp of olive oil. Process until smooth. (Makes about 1/2 cup.)

Chilled buckwheat soba noodles are traditionally served as a cooling meal in the heat of summer in Japan. In this salad, the noodles are tossed with plenty of veggies, grilled chicken and a gingery dressing. 

RD Tip: Be sure to buy noodles with a high percentage of whole-grain buckwheat flour for the most beneficial fiber.

Active time: 35 minutes | Total time: 45 minutes

Soba Noodle Salad With Ginger Dressing

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 tbsp honey
  • 2 tsp toasted sesame oil
  • 3 tsp ginger, finely grated
  • 3 medium garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp canola oil
  • 3 cups (85g) shiitake mushrooms, stems discarded, caps sliced
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper
  • 2 (8 oz/227g each) boneless, skinless chicken breasts
  • 4 cups (227g) broccoli crowns, cut into 2-inch florets
  • 1/2 cup (75g) shelled edamame
  • 6 oz (170g) dry buckwheat soba noodles 
  • 3 green onions, thinly sliced

Directions

In a small bowl, combine the soy sauce, vinegar, honey, sesame oil, ginger and garlic. Set aside.

Heat the canola oil in a cast-iron skillet or heavy sauté pan over medium-high heat. Add the mushrooms and sauté until tender, 4 minutes. Transfer to a large serving bowl. Season the chicken with the salt and pepper before adding it to the skillet, reduce the heat to medium, and cook until cooked through and an instant-read thermometer registers 165°F (74°C), about 5 minutes per side. With a slotted spoon, transfer the vegetables to the serving bowl with the mushrooms. Add the soba noodles to the boiling water and cook according to package instructions. Drain and rinse with cool water to stop the noodles from overcooking. Add the noodles to the vegetables in the bowl. Toss with the soy sauce dressing.

Thinly slice the chicken against the grain and place on top of the noodle mixture. Sprinkle green onions over the top and serve. (The salad can be stored in an airtight container for up to 2 days.)

The ‘Tavern-Style’ Pizza Takeover

📅 Oct 20, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →
The thin-crust pizzas popular throughout the Midwest have taken off as the “tavern-style” pizza trend | Brad Bobrow/La Merna

In the suburbs of Chicago where they both grew up, it was just pizza, according to Brittany and Brennan Grosvenor, the couple behind Proof in Portland, Oregon. Ordering came down to the question of “thin-crust, deep-dish, or double-dough,” Brittany recalls of her family’s Friday night tradition. The Grosvernors sell the former at Proof, though the language of “thin crust” has given way to the more nationally familiar phrasing of “tavern-style,” the pizza trend that has swept from coast to coast in recent years. 

Outsiders might associate Chicago with its famous, tourist-beckoning deep-dish pizza, but Brittany says that’s just not reflective of reality, citing deep dish as a much less frequent treat than the Midwestern, thin-crust style that’s gaining popularity. “The pizza you get every week, or if you go to a kid’s birthday party, it’s always tavern-style,” she says.

“Tavern-style” pizza — which is prevalent  across the Midwest, with Chicago, Milwaukee, Minnesota, and St. Louis touting similar pies — is known for its thin, cracker-like crust, cut into a lattice instead of triangular slices. Depending on the region and the pizza shop where one’s nostalgia lies, the sauce might lean sweet or spicy. Lightness and crispness, though, is the style’s unique sell. Writing for Esquire in 2023, Omar Mamoon called the variant, which was then still on the rise, “a foil” to famously thick Detroit-style pizza, which had its big moment in the late 2010s. 

The beauty of the tavern-style pie is its shareability. A pizza yields more slices when cut into a grid than into triangles, explains Bradley Shorten, who’s behind both Kim’s Uncle Pizza in his hometown Chicago suburb of Westmont, Illinois, and the recently opened La Merna in Washington, D.C. Pizza cut into little squares makes for great drinking food, especially since it fits on a cocktail napkin. What makes a good pie, according to Shorten, is that every square has a little of every ingredient; the toppings go to the edge. And while the four, small corner slices might not be the “best” ones, Shorten says, they’re the ones “everybody goes for,” especially if they’re from Chicago.

Over the past few years, as “taverns” have become growing fixations for a cadre of restaurateurs, the “party cut” pizza and tavern-style pizza spots have also become ubiquitous, with openings like Keepers in New York city; One Trick Pony in Forth Worth, Texas; Tillie’s Tavern Pizza in Wheat Ridge, Colorado; and Zarella and Dimmi Dimmi Corner Italian in Chicago, where the style has always been abundant. For some Chicagoans, these openings across the country are an opportunity to correct the record about their city’s pizza culture, and for other Midwesterners to claim their stake in the region’s pizza, too.

When Mike Fadem opened the second location of his popular NYC pizzeria Ops — known for its wood-fired Neapolitan-esque pies with leopard-spotted crusts and pliant dough — he wanted to offer something new. Fadem is from St. Louis, where the city’s namesake pizza is super-thin. Despite his love for it, “it’s maybe not the best version of tavern pizza,” Fadem admits, noting the iteration’s many critics, some of whom take issue with its use of the gooey processed cheese called provel. The trajectory of tavern-style pizza has been like a game of telephone, he explains: St. Louis pizza is derived from thin-crust Chicago pizza, which itself came from Neapolitan pizza. Divergence has accompanied each jump.

One of Fadem’s employees at Ops, meanwhile, is from New Jersey, which claims its own thin-crust, tavern- or bar-style pizza tradition. (The bar pies of the Boston area also have their own devoted following.) Accordingly, “I had this interest in ‘can we make an Ops version of this?’” Fadem says. With Ops known for sourdough, Fadem also wanted to highlight the rich flavor of whole grains. And he wanted it to be really crispy, a quality he says draws in New Yorkers. Fadem’s take is on the menu as the regionally agnostic “tavernetta.” Though St. Louis and New Jersey served as initial inspiration, the best pizza from his research was actually in Milwaukee. The Tavernetta hasn’t displaced the other pies at the restaurant; it’s ordered in addition to them, with most groups getting at least one.

In June, Los Angeles cocktail spot Bar Next Door celebrated its third anniversary by adding food to its menu. Previously, the bar had allowed customers to bring in pies from Prince Street Pizza next door since Prince Street co-owner Lawrence Longo is a partner in it. “But, you know, after having a slice, sometimes you get a little full and you’re like, I want to go to bed,” says owner and bar director Brynn Smith. “That’s the whole goal for us: for people to stay a little longer, drink more. We thought the tavern-style pizza would be perfect.” The fact that the pizzas — they’re technically listed on the menu as “bar pies,” to better go with the establishment’s name — are made exclusively for the bar by Prince Street just adds another reason for people to go out, Smith says.

The idea of the tavern as a social and convivial space is inseparable from the tavern-style pizza. Shorten’s goal with La Merna — which he runs with his wife Meg Schmitt, who grew up in St. Louis — was the feel of a Chicago dive bar or a Wisconsin supper club: a place with wood-paneled walls and checkered floors, where people could slide into a red leather booth for pitchers of beer and pizza and hang out for a while. As my colleague Francky Knapp wrote last year, the rising appeal of the “tavern” vibe across restaurants nationwide is rooted in the sense of providing somewhere homey and lived-in. At taverns, diners are beckoned to sprawl out and stay awhile; to free themselves of pretense and to tap into something welcoming in its familiarity. This, for so many people, is exemplified by the archetype of hometown pizza shop, which is forever tethered in our cultural imaginations to the trappings of a previous, simpler era.

It’s for this reason that, in Oregon, the Grosvenors have started serving their take on Bosco sticks, the stuffed breadsticks of Midwestern cafeterias, and Italian beef with giardiniera. While they could find certain Midwestern hits around Portland, it sometimes frustrated them that they couldn’t find everything in one place. According to Brennan Grosvenor, “We’re just trying to embody the feeling: If you went into a tavern in Chicago, what could you order there?”

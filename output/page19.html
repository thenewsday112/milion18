Pizza romana e piatti di pesce insieme a Fiumicino: niente più litigi

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Luca Pezzetta ne ha combinata un’altra delle sue, e ha aperto Casa Clementina a Fiumicino, nei locali che prima occupava Ippolito.

La trattoria contemporanea ha lasciato il posto a un format probabilmente più in linea con il suo stile immediato e riconoscibile, basato sulla ricerca di materia prima, tecnica precisa e abbinamenti golosi.

Il locale si affaccia sulla passerella pedonale e sui pescherecci di Fiumicino, con tavoli interni e uno spazioso dehors. Il nome vuole rievocare un’atmosfera domestica, informale, con il servizio e i dettagli che rimandano a una tavola di casa. E ricette che ricordano i pranzi della domenica, delle feste, delle occasioni, buoni e generosi.

Casa Clementina vuole evitare litigi per la scelta tra ristorante e pizzeria. E quindi, si può mangiare pizza e pesce allo stesso tavolo senza che una delle due opzioni venga relegata al ruolo di comparsa?

Luca Pezzetta e Simone Curti, due mestieri al tavolo di Casa Clementina

La risposta è sì e a ciò contribuisce in larga parte Simone Curti, cuoco di mare con una lunga storia sul litorale.

Ha lavorato da Pascucci al Porticciolo, stella Michelin a Fiumicino. E si è fatto conoscere dal pubblico con il suo Molo 17 a Ostia, locale di riferimento per chi cercava vera cucina di pesce. Da Casa Clementina, come allora, lavora sulla filiera cortissima del pescato di Fiumicino e sulla stagionalità, con cotture che non devono nascondere niente.

La storia di Casa Clementina parte da una scelta di Luca Pezzetta. Dopo cinque anni di pizzeria Clementina voleva dare una casa nuova alla sua pizza, e un’offerta meno separata. “Alla stessa tavola non ci sono più fazioni”, racconta, “si può ordinare la pizza ma anche scegliere fra i piatti di pesce”. Gli impasti sono gli stessi di sempre, leggeri e ad alta idratazione, più lievitati e fermentazioni. Tra l’altro il locale viene incontro a chi vorrebbe la pizza anche a pranzo (Clementina pizzeria è aperta solo a cena tranne che nei weekend).

A cena a Casa Clementina

Complici le temperature ancora miti, ci accomodiamo all’aperto per questa cena stampa. Siamo in compagnia dello sciabordio dell’acqua del canale poco distante che riempiva gli spazi lasciati dalle voci zittite dal cibo.

Arriva il cestino del pane, e – visto che siamo nel regno di Pezzetta – ci buttiamo con fiducia. Il bun al burro è goloso, ancora più morbido servito tiepido, mentre la fetta di casareccio profuma di casa.

Nell’attesa, la pizza al padellino con i pachino schiacciati ci fa salivare. Difficile scegliere tra gli antipasti, ciascuno vede un ingrediente principale esaltato dai condimenti ideali. Optiamo per l’insalata di mare, più che altro per sfida, visto che è una proposta semplice solo in apparenza (16 €). Arriva tiepida e condita quel tanto che basta a vestire di velo seppioline, totani, polpo, molluschi dolci e morbidi che si giocano la partita con verdure julienne croccanti e olive saporite. Proprio come dovrebbe essere l’insalata di mare.

I piatti

Il piatto arriva bello caldo e fumante, come appena spadellato dal fornello accanto alla tavola. Il servizio è fattore determinante dell’illusione domestica: è veloce, si palesa al momento giusto, è amichevole.

Altra concessione di gola, la frittura di mare, qui di calamari e gamberi (18 €). Forse scomodo da mangiare, che lascia le dita unte, ma delizioso. Niente da eccepire, i calamari e i gamberi di Casa Clementina sono ottimi, profumati, freschissimi.

The Games We Couldn’t Stop Playing In October 2026

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

All eyes may be on Grand Theft Auto 6's release in November, but this fall has kicked off with plenty else to play. Be it a new friendslop horror game, a relaxing driving experience, or a Mission: Impossible-style deception simulator, we've been enjoying a wide range of games in October.

As usual, you can check out our staff picks for October below. We're primarily focused on recent releases that we loved and you might not be familiar with, though we've had some better-known games dominating our free time, too. 

Escape from Playtime

The "friendslop" genre is dominated by horror games, and the latest of them is a new favorite for me and my daughter. Escape from Playtime is an eight-player co-op game set in the Poppy Playtime universe, combining the climbing mechanics of Peak with the quota-chasing loop of REPO, all set in a liminal world inspired by the Playrooms. The Poppy Playtime story is strangely complex, but you don't need to understand any of it to enjoy this offshoot.

My little girl loves horror, so creepy mascot vibes like those in Escape from Playtime are right up our alley--for now, at least. After we played it for a bit, she saw in the trailer that we may eventually run into Miss Delight (pictured above) and is now having second thoughts. We'll see how much deeper into the weird world of Poppy we can get before she 180s for the exit. -- Mark Delaney

See at Steam

Lego Batman's Mayhem Mode

Lego Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight was one of my favorites earlier this year, a fun mash-up romp through various Batman continuity with combat inspired by the Arkham series. The new Mayhem Mode is a treat--a quick retelling of Joker and Harley's toxic romance followed by a new roguelike mode which has the clown couple wreak havoc across Gotham on a strict time limit. 

You only have three minutes to complete objectives like setting up Joker's iconic balloons from the 1989 film, all while extending your timer by collecting studs and completing optional objectives. After the three minutes are up, you either escape with all the loot or get captured by the Bat and give up a huge chunk of it. Task Force X is also on your tail, which means you'll sometimes get random encounters against antiheroes like Deadshot, King Shark, and Captain Boomerang. 

With your ill-gotten gains, you can collect a wide variety of Joker and Harley outfits from across their many incarnations, scratching that same collector nostalgia itch as the main game. It's a bit slight, but it's fun to have a reason to revisit Lego Gotham City. -- Steve Watts

See at Fanatical

Gears of War: E-Day

It saddens me that a game from a series this huge would be under the radar for anyone, but early player figures for Gears of War: E-Day have not been particularly impressive. That's a real shame, because it's up there with my favorite games of the year. The prequel chronicles the opening days of the Locust invasion, with Marcus and Dom's ragtag squad forced to improvise as the homicidal creatures begin decimating Sera.

Alongside all the classic third-person shooting and giant bosses you'd expect from a Gears game, there are also plenty of quiet world-building moments that ended up being some of my favorite parts. I'm curious to see where The Coalition will take the story next, because we were left on a major cliffhanger at the end of Gears 5, but I also really want to know what happened to the (still-alive) characters introduced in E-Day. For that to happen, however, people need to play this game, so go do that. -- Gabe Gurwin

See at Steam

Also playing: 

  • Warhammer 40,000: Boltgun 2: The sequel to an already-wonderful boomer shooter is somehow even better, with a new playable character, branching paths, and more varied enemies. 

Legion Was Here

In an age of fragmented attention spans, Legion Was Here might just be the perfect way to train your brain muscles. As someone who religiously watches Columbo reruns, a game that's all about observing the small details is one that appeals heavily to me, especially when you're challenged to come up with the perfect disguise, memorize every minute fact about your target, and infiltrate a clandestine meeting where one slip-up could get you killed. 

The work of a solo developer, Legion Was Here merges the thrill of creating a custom character, espionage, and talking to random people, creating one of the best high-stakes experiences of the year. Quirky art, nuanced interrogations, and the thrill of successfully coming up with the perfect disguise makes for one short and sweet game. -- Darryn Bonthuys

See at Steam

Over the Hill

I often find "relaxing" games to just be pretty boring. There are exceptions, and Over the Hill is the latest: a game where you drive around in various classic-style 4x4s, exploring beautiful landscapes and snapping pictures, and not doing much else.

The vibes are very chill, but Over the Hill has maintained my attention with the style of its driving. Traversing its off-road areas, you'll need to rely on your winch and gear, make intelligent use of low/high-gear, and generally be mindful of the paths you take. There is a deliberateness with which you move through the world--you won't get 200mph streaks across the map like in Forza Horizon--that simultaneously makes for engaging gameplay and contributes to the relaxing nature of the game.

Automatic/manual transmission lets you tweak just how locked in you need to be, but for me, using automatic allows Over the Hill to find that sweet spot of letting me relax without ever feeling boring. -- Chris Pereira

See at Fanatical

Also playing:

  • Forbidden Solitaire's The Discarded DLC -- A great excuse to revisit one of the year's best games.
  • My Arms Are Longer Now demo -- An absurd comedy game that also, oddly, got me to just chill out.
  • Dog in the Machine -- Into the Breach is an all-time favorite of mine, and I'm having fun in the early going with this take on it that centers around robotic dogs.

The Doll Shop

Though The Doll Shop is short and sounds sweet, the reality is that this game is quite bitter and extremely dark. In this lushly illustrated visual novel, you play as an isolated young man who has taken over his father's doll shop. After repairing a doll that seen better days, our protagonist realizes he never received the address of his client, Mrs. Fujimoto, and sets out onto the snow-covered streets of his Japanese hometown to find someone who might know where she lives.

However, each person he encounters seems corrupted--shrouded in black static save for their eerily smiles and pale, vacant eyes. We also come to discover there has been a disappearance in town, as fliers related to the disappearance of a young girl hang in every window. Over four days, our protagonist is forced to dig deep to discover what is going on in this small town.

As a lover of psychological horror, painterly art direction, multiple endings, and games I can play through in one sitting, The Doll Shop checked a lot of boxes for me. Though not as robust, frightening, or emotional as Paranormasight or Slay the Princess, I'd recommend the experience to fans of either--especially if they're looking for a tame-yet-disturbing game to play this spooky season. -- Jessica Cogswell

See at Steam

Also playing:

  • Queen's Domain -- After finishing its fantastic demo, I am thrilled to play more of this stylish, dark fantasy RPG inspired by From Software's King's Field.

Every time you slide behind the wheel of a modern electric vehicle or hybrid, you are interacting with thousands of engineering decisions designed to maximize efficiency and longevity. Drivers often focus obsessively on battery degradation rates or tire alignment parameters to squeeze out extra driving range. The exterior shell of the vehicle rarely receives the same level of analytical attention. The coating on your vehicle serves as the primary defense mechanism against a harsh daily environment of road debris, ultraviolet radiation and chemical contaminants. A well-maintained exterior preserves the underlying metal and composite materials while keeping the sleek aesthetic of the vehicle intact.

The Role of Smooth Exterior Surfaces in Vehicle Longevity

Photo by Michael Coates

Automotive design has shifted dramatically toward aerodynamic efficiency. Automakers spend countless hours in wind tunnels smoothing out body lines to reduce drag coefficients. While a tiny rock chip will not drastically reduce your total driving range, ignoring surface damage can lead to cascading structural issues. Moisture and road salt easily penetrate exposed areas where the protective layers have been compromised. This creates micro-environments where oxidation thrives. When factory coatings wear down or sustain damage, applying high-quality car paint ensures the protective barrier remains intact against moisture and road salt. Addressing small imperfections promptly stops rust before it can spread beneath the surface and cause expensive panel damage.

Spotting and Addressing Minor Surface Damage Early

Developing a routine habit of inspecting your vehicle after long road trips or harsh weather events is a highly practical strategy. Most damage occurs low on the front bumper or along the rocker panels where tires kick up debris from the road. You should look for small white or gray spots that indicate a chip has penetrated down to the primer. Catching these blemishes early means the repair process remains simple and highly localized.

Understanding the Layers of Protection

Modern automotive finishes consist of a sophisticated multi-layer system. The bare metal or plastic is first coated with a primer that creates a bonding surface and prevents corrosion. The base coat sits on top of this primer and provides the actual color of the vehicle. Finally, a clear coat seals the base layer to provide ultraviolet protection and a glossy finish. Knowing which layer has been damaged dictates the complexity of the repair. A light scratch that only scuffs the clear coat can often be polished away with a gentle abrasive compound. A chip that reveals bare metal requires replacement of all three foundational layers.

Choosing the Right Materials for a Seamless Repair

Photo by Lex Adams

Tackling deeper chips and scratches requires matching the repair materials to the specific type of damage. Small rock chips are easily handled with a precision touch-up pen that deposits a tiny amount of color directly into the crater. Larger scrapes along a bumper might necessitate aerosol application to blend the new finish smoothly into the surrounding area. Upgrading to a two-component clear coat system adds a chemical hardener that mimics the durability of a factory finish. For those looking to restore a panel to its original factory finish, utilizing specialized products like CROP car paint provides the precise color match and durability required for a professional-grade repair. Taking the time to locate your vehicle’s exact factory color code ensures the new application blends invisibly into the existing finish.

The Importance of Surface Preparation

Even the most accurate color formulation will fail if the underlying surface is not prepared correctly. Automotive body panels must be entirely free of wax, grease and road grime before any new material is applied. A dedicated silicone remover or degreaser strips away these invisible contaminants. Lightly sanding the damaged area creates a textured mechanical profile that allows the new coating to grip the surface firmly. Rushing the preparation phase almost always results in peeling or flaking down the road.

Best Practices for Applying Touch-ups at Home

Patience is your most valuable tool when repairing exterior damage. You should always work in a well-ventilated, dust-free environment with moderate temperatures. Extreme heat causes materials to dry too quickly and leads to uneven textures. Cold temperatures prevent proper curing and leave the finish soft and vulnerable. Applying multiple thin layers rather than one heavy coat prevents runs and allows the solvents to evaporate properly between applications.

Once the base color has dried completely, adding the protective clear coat restores the original depth and shine. The repaired area will usually look slightly raised compared to the surrounding panel. Allowing the fresh application to cure for several days gives you the opportunity to lightly wet-sand and polish the spot until it sits perfectly flush with the rest of the body. Mastering this straightforward maintenance process protects your automotive investment and keeps your vehicle looking factory fresh for years to come.

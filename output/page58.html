Abbracci are classic Italian cookies made with a vanilla cream and chocolate dough shaped together to form a hug. This is my version of the iconic Mulino Bianco cookies, often eaten for breakfast in Italy. They’re buttery, crumbly, and absolutely delicious.

Recipe overview

  • The recipe: Classic Italian two-tone breakfast cookies inspired by Mulino Bianco Abbracci.
  • Texture and taste: Buttery, crumbly cookies with a taste of vanilla and chocolate (not overly sweet).
  • Total time: 2 hours 40 minutes (including chilling time)
  • Makes: around 40 cookies.
  • Best for: Breakfast or a snack.
  • Make-ahead: The dough will keep well in the fridge for 2-3 days before baking.
Jump to recipe

Mulino Bianco is one of Italy’s best-known brands for making breakfast cookies. There are so many different varieties to choose from, and they are hugely popular.

My daughter loves these cookies, so I thought it would be fun to recreate them at home, and the first one I chose to recreate was abbracci.

Abbracci, meaning ‘hugs’ in Italian, are made with two different doughs. A sweet Panna (cream) dough that wraps around a delicious chocolate dough to create their classic ‘hug’ shape.

They’re buttery, sweet, crumbly and perfect for dunking in your morning cappuccino or enjoying as a snack.

Ingredient notes

  • Italian 00 flour: this is my go-to flour for most of my Italian baking, and it’s what I’ve used here, although all-purpose flour is perfectly fine to use too.
  • Butter: use unsalted room temperature butter.
  • Eggs: make sure they are at room temperature.
  • Vanilla: I use good quality extract, but a vanilla bean or vanilla bean paste can also be used.
  • Heavy cream (double cream UK): I developed the recipe with cream because it’s in the original Mulino Bianco recipe. It adds moisture and richness, but you can replace it with whole milk instead.
  • Honey: this is an ingredient that features in a lot of Mulino Bianco’s breakfast cookies. Make sure you use runny honey and not a firm/solid variety.

My tips for making Abbracci Cookies

Use a kitchen scale: I highly recommend using a scale for this recipe. Weighing the dough to divide it in half and then portion the cookies is really important for an even size and bake.

Cookie portion size: I found 10g of each cookie dough (20g total) to be the perfect size. I did test these with 15g pieces of dough, and they were too big and took too long to bake, so this is another reason I recommend using a scale.

Space the cookies out: the cookies do spread slightly in the oven, so make sure to space them out on your baking tray (around 1/2 inch apart).

Chill the dough twice: the cookie dough must be chilled before shaping. I recommend chilling the dough for at least 1 hour so it’s very firm. You then need to chill the cookies for around 20 minutes before baking, so they don’t spread too much in the oven.

My butter and sugar have split, what do I do?

Don’t worry if your mixture looks like it has split or curdled. The reason is that the butter and sugar haven’t been creamed together for long enough (I usually do 5 minutes), or your eggs have cooled the butter down too much (they should be at room temp).

If this happens, do not worry; the mixture will come together again once you add the flour, and it won’t affect the cookies (this happened a few times to me during the testing process, and there’s no issue).

Can I add more honey and less sugar?

This was something I tested because ideally I would have rathered more honey and less sugar in a breakfast cookie. I found that increasing the honey caused the cookies to turn a deeper golden colour, which I did not want. It also adds more moisture and makes the cookies less crumbly, so I found that two teaspoons is the perfect amount.

Italian Abbracci Cookies (chocolate and cream)

Use the camera icon to toggle the step by step photos on and off. Italian abbracci cookies made with a buttery cream, vanilla and chocolate dough. They're crumbly, delicious and perfect for breakfast or as a snack. Course cookies Cuisine Italian Prep Time 1 hour hour Cook Time 19 minutes minutes chilling time 1 hour hour 20 minutes minutes Total Time 2 hours hours 39 minutes minutes Servings 40 cookies Calories 90kcal Author Emily Wyper

Equipment

  • Stand mixer with paddle attachment
  • Kitchen scale
  • Plastic wrap (cling film)
  • silicone spatula
  • Mixing bowls
  • Baking tray
  • Parchment paper

Ingredients

Base dough

  • 185 g (13 tablespoons) unsalted butter (at room temperature)
  • 130 g (2/3 cup) caster sugar (superfine) or use granulated
  • 2 teaspoons runny honey
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 2 large egg yolks (at room temperature)
  • 1 large egg (at room temperature)
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
  • Pinch of salt

Panna (cream) dough

  • 210 g (1 1/2 cups) Italian 00 flour (or all-purpose flour)
  • 1 tablespoon heavy cream (double cream UK)

Cocoa dough

  • 30 g (3 1/2 tablespoons) cocoa powder
  • 180 g (1 1/4 cup plus 1 teaspoon) Italian 00 flour (or all-purpose flour)

Instructions

To make the dough

  • First, put the butter and sugar in the bowl of a stand mixer with a paddle attachment and beat them together until light and fluffy (about 5 minutes).
  • Whisk the whole egg and 2 egg yolks together in a small bowl, then with the mixer running slowly, pour the eggs in one third at a time, letting the mixture absorb the egg before adding more.
  • Once the egg is fully incorporated, add the baking powder, vanilla, honey and a pinch of salt. Mix until just combined.
  • Put a large mixing bowl on your kitchen scale and weigh the whole mixture. Use a spatula to scrape out as much as possible. Once you know the weight of the whole mixture, divide the mixture in half into two separate mixing bowls.
  • Add the 30g cocoa powder and 180g flour to one mixing bowl and 1 tablespoon of heavy cream and 210g flour to the other mixing bowl.
  • Working with one batch of dough at a time, use a fork to mix the dough until large crumbs form. Then use your hands to bring the dough together and shape it into a large disc.
  • Wrap the dough in plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 1 hour.

Shape the cookies

  • Once the cookie dough is chilled and firm, remove it from the fridge. Cut a small piece of cocoa dough and weigh out 10g, then roll it into a small ball. Do the same with the panna dough, so you have one 10g ball of each.
  • Next, roll each ball into a small log around 2.5 inches (6cm) in length. Shape each one into a ‘C’ shape, then gently place the panna dough over the cocoa dough at the ends and press down to join them together to create the classic abbracci shape.
  • Arrange the cookies on a lined baking tray about 1/2 inch apart, then refrigerate them for 20 minutes. I usually do this in batches to save space.

Bake

  • Preheat the oven to 170℃/340℉ conventional (static).
  • Once chilled, bake the cookies for 19 minutes. Let them cool in the tray, then move to a cooling rack. Store in an airtight container.

Notes

  • Storage: The cookies will keep well in an airtight container for 1-2 weeks.

15 years of Port Rowan

📅 Aug 29, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

My calendar app reminded me that on this day in August, 2011, I embarked on what would be my most successful layout-building project.

A short freight leaves St. Williams, bound for Port Rowan.

That layout was Port Rowan in 1:64 – an S scale interpretation of a CNR branch line in its final years.

On paper, the layout was simple: It filled an odd-shaped space roughly 14 by 35 feet, but featured just two towns (plus staging) and a grand total of eight turnouts. The layout supported one train at a time operation, with enough work to keep a two-person crew entertained for an hour or so. It’s the very definition of what I came to call an Achievable Layout.

But there was a lot more to Port Rowan than that description suggests.

I have hovered around the prototype modelling sphere for decades and have built (well, started) a number of prototype-based layouts. But Port Rowan marked the first time I had built a layout that looked like a model of a railway, rather than a model railway. By exercising restraint during track planning, I ended up with plenty of room to present the railway in context with its surroundings.

I loved that about it.

In a scene that would never have happened on the prototype, two trains meet at Port Rowan. Note how scenery dominates the scene: I loved how I was able to present the railway as part of a larger world.

Port Rowan’s appearance encouraged me to put the railway in context operationally, too. I embraced what I call “finescale operations”, with prototypically-designed paperwork, a working telegraph system, turnouts controlled by switch stands, and other features. It was very satisfying.

As I reflect on what made it such a success – for me, anyway – I’m considering what lessons from Port Rowan I can apply to my current layouts. Overall, I’m pleased with GWR Bydemill and CNR Pine Street. But I think some improvements are in order for my main layout – based on the Niagara St. Catharines & Toronto Railway.

The NS&T is the project that is closest to my heart, and as a consequence it’s the subject on which I have placed the greatest demands. As I look at it today, I realize the layout I’m building is not measuring up. Changes are in order, and Port Rowan provides me with important insights into what those should be.

Roasted, marinated bell peppers made with garlic, fresh basil, balsamic vinegar and olive oil. This recipe is super simple and makes the most deliciously sweet and flavourful peppers that can be used in so many ways.

I absolutely love these marinated bell peppers. Not only are they super easy to make, but they are so versatile.

Roasting the peppers until soft and lightly charred gives them a beautifully sweet flavour. Once peeled, they’re marinated in garlic, basil, balsamic vinegar, and olive oil, which balances the sweetness perfectly.

They make such a great antipasto alongside some bread, olives, cured meats and cheese or (my personal favourite), add them to thick focaccia sandwiches. I use my homemade focaccia dough and usually opt for rocket/arugula, cheese and marinated peppers.

They are delicious in salads, or as a side dish to chicken or fish; the list is endless!

Ingredient notes

  • Bell peppers – I love to use a mix of red and yellow bell peppers, but you could choose just one if you prefer.
  • Balsamic vinegar – try to go for a mid-range balsamic vinegar so not the cheapest but also nothing crazy expensive. I spend around £8/$11 on a bottle that’s good quality. It should be slightly thick and sweet (I go into more detail in our pantry essentials)
  • Garlic – always use fresh garlic. I find that 1 large clove is enough, but add more if you prefer.
  • Basil – use fresh when it comes to herbs too, especially for a simple dish like this; it makes all the difference!
  • Olive oil – use a good quality, peppery extra virgin olive oil which will add so much flavour to the marinade.

Other flavour options

You can switch up the flavour of these marinated peppers depending on what you’re serving them with or what you feel like. Here are some ideas

  • Add oregano (I recommend fresh if possible, but dried is fine here too)
  • Add fresh parsley or mint
  • Add anchovies (I like to lay these on top, but you could also mash 1-2 up into the dressing).
  • Add capers (in brine)
  • Red wine vinegar (instead of balsamic)

Other ways to cook your bell peppers

There are three main ways to cook your bell peppers, and all of them work really well.

  1. Roasting: this is the method we use, which is super easy with less hands-on prep time involved.
  2. BBQ: you can char your peppers (whole) on a BBQ during the summer months, which is a great option if you don’t want to turn on the oven. It will also give the peppers a more intense smoky flavour.
  3. Gas stove or grill/broiler: you can opt to char the peppers all over under your broiler/oven grill or use your gas stove. This will give you a nice smoky flavour but is more hands-on prep as you have to watch them until they are charred all over.

Italian Roasted Marinated Peppers

Use the camera icon to toggle the step by step photos on and off. Deliciously sweet marinated bell peppers made with garlic, basil and balsamic vinegar. Served as a side dish, on salads or on sandwiches. Course Side Dish Cuisine Italian Prep Time 15 minutes minutes Cook Time 30 minutes minutes cooling and marinating time 1 hour hour Total Time 1 hour hour 45 minutes minutes Servings 4 – 6 servings Calories 100kcal Author Emily Wyper

Equipment

  • Plastic wrap (cling film)

Ingredients

  • 2 red bell peppers (medium-sized)
  • 2 yellow bell peppers (medium-sized)
  • 1 garlic clove
  • 1 small handful fresh basil
  • 2 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil plus extra for roasting
  • 1.5 tablespoons balsamic vinegar (good quality)
  • Salt and pepper

Instructions

  • Preheat the oven to 200℃/400℉ fan or 220℃/425℉ conventional.
  • Lightly drizzle the peppers in olive oil and place them on a baking tray. Roast them in the oven for 30-35 minutes, until soft and charred.
  • Place the peppers in a large bowl and cover it with plastic wrap (cling film). Let the peppers cool for at least 30 minutes, but you can also wait until they are completely cold.
  • Once cooled, remove the seeds and stalks from the peppers and peel off the skin. This should come away very easily.
  • Cut the peppers into strips and arrange them on a serving plate or dish and season them with a pinch of salt.
  • Finely slice the garlic and add it on top of the peppers, then scatter over some torn basil leaves.
  • Combine the olive oil and balsamic vinegar with a good pinch of salt and pepper, then whisk it together until combined. Pour the dressing over the peppers, then cover with plastic wrap or foil and leave to marinate for at least 30 minutes before serving.
  • Tip: The peppers taste best once they have been left to marinate for a few hours.

Notes

  • Storage: The peppers can be stored in the fridge for 3-4 days. Bring them to room temperature for at least 30 minutes before serving for best flavour.

These golden-crusted tempeh balls are a variation on the ones I included in my last book. They’re nutritionally packed with beans, grated tempeh and shredded veggies, brightened with a splash of ponzu. You can enjoy them on their own, or as part of a larger meal. I had them here as part of a bento-style lunch alongside some wild rice, leftover baked quinoa patties, and edamame. I also like them as a stand-in for traditional meatballs with spaghetti and this mushroom ragù. 

Variations

I play around quite a bit with the make-up of these tempeh balls. Keep the ratio of beans to tempeh intact and then experiment with the rest. A few past successes include:

  • Spicy: If I’m going to go spicy, I tend to introduce a spicy component through something like this chili crisp. Opting for a drizzle of that in place of the ponzu. That said, you can absolutely spice the actual tempeh balls themselves if you like. Use a bit of chili powder, crushed red pepper flakes, or work a favorite curry paste into the mixture.
  • Herb-filled: Swap out the cabbage for chopped soft, green herbs. A blend of chives, dill, cilantro, and basil are all good candidates. I like to trade out the onion for chopped scallions in this version.
  • Super Green: Use finely chopped kale, spinach, and/or chard in place of the corn/carrots and cabbage.

Pan-Fried Tempeh Ponzu Balls: Step-by-step

These are a breeze to make. The first thing you’re going to do is puree the beans and then combine them in a big bowl with the other ingredients (photo below). As I mention above, I used corn for this version because it’s in season, but you can use carrots.

The key to the next step is getting in there with your hands. You really want to mash and smash and squeeze the mixture together so it’s uniform and all the ingredients are distributed equally (see below).

To shape the tempeh balls, use a tablespoon and your hands. If things get too tacky or messy, rinse your hands and reboot. Keeping your hands damp and clean-ish throughout the process is helpful.

The last step is pan-frying the tempeh balls. My one pro-tip is to let them get a good amount of color on each side before you start to move them around the pan. This way the tempeh mixture is less likely to stick to the pan.

I typically serve these with ponzu sauce. It is quite salty, so I under-season the ponzu balls a bit. If you’re going to serve them with something else, something not as assertive on the salt front, go ahead and adjust the tempeh mixture with more, until it tastes good to you.

Roasted Cherry Tomato and Ricotta Pasta. Made with a pinch of chilli flakes, lots of garlic and fresh basil. It’s creamy, full of flavour, and so easy to make.

This is such a simple pasta recipe that the whole family loves. My 3-year-old devours it every time, and it couldn’t be easier.

Roast the cherry tomatoes with plenty of garlic and a pinch of chilli flakes, if desired, until they are sweet, jammy and slightly charred. Blend them with ricotta and Parmigiano cheese, and it’s pretty much done.

The ricotta creates a delicious creamy sauce without adding too much richness, while the tomatoes add so much flavour once roasted.

I’ve listed some substitutions in the ingredient notes below so you can use whatever you have on hand. This is a great recipe to have in your back pocket for a simple weeknight dinner.

Ingredient notes and substitutions

  • Cherry tomatoes: I like cherry or grape tomatoes for their sweetness, but you can use a mix of other tomatoes. I’ll often throw in a few larger tomatoes, roughly chopped, that are hanging around my kitchen.
  • Garlic: I use 3 large cloves. You can use more or less if you prefer. When garlic is roasted, the flavour mellows a lot so the sauce doesn’t have a pungent garlic flavour.
  • Ricotta: Instead of ricotta, you could use heavy cream, mascarpone or even a tangy soft cheese such as stracchino.
  • Chilli flakes (not pictured): I add a tiny pinch (1/4 teaspoon), which adds a subtle kick and warmth (my 3-year-old eats it with this, so it’s not spicy). You can opt to leave them out, add more or even roast fresh red chillies with the tomatoes instead.
  • Spaghetti: spaghetti or linguine work really well with this sauce, but most short pasta shapes would also work well.
  • Parmigiano Reggiano: Pecorino Romano would also work really well!

My top tip!

Emulsify the sauce: this is my biggest tip for this recipe. The consistency of the sauce after blending is quite thick, so you want to loosen it with pasta water.

I add around 120ml (1/2 cup) while the pasta is cooking, then add more if needed once the pasta has been added. Toss everything together until the sauce is smooth, creamy and coats the spaghetti well.

Roasted Cherry Tomato and Ricotta Pasta

Use the camera icon to toggle the step by step photos on and off. Creamy roasted tomato and ricotta pasta with a pinch of chilli flakes, lots of garlic and fresh basil. Delicious and super easy! Course pasta Cuisine Italian Prep Time 15 minutes minutes Cook Time 35 minutes minutes Total Time 50 minutes minutes Servings 4 servings Calories 498kcal Author Emily Wyper

Equipment

  • Baking tray
  • Blender

Ingredients

  • 1/3 cup (20g) Parmigiano Reggiano (finely grated) plus extra for serving
  • 1 small handful fresh basil
  • 400 g (14oz) Spaghetti or other dried pasta
  • Olive oil
  • Salt and pepper

Instructions

  • Preheat the oven to 180℃/350℉fan or 200℃/400℉ static.
  • Cut the cherry tomatoes in half and place them on a baking tray. Add the 3 cloves of garlic (keep the skins on) then drizzle over 2-3 tablespoons of olive oil. Arrange the tomatoes so they are facing cut side up.
  • Sprinkle over the chilli flakes and a good pinch of salt and pepper then roast in the oven for around 25 minutes until soft, jammy and slightly charred.
  • Bring a large pot of water to a boil and salt it well.
  • Once roasted, transfer the cherry tomatoes to a blender. Remove the skins from the garlic and add that to the blender too alongside the ricotta and grated Parmigiano Reggiano.
  • Blitz until smooth. Taste for seasoning and add more salt if needed. Transfer the sauce to a pan and add a small handful of torn basil leaves.
  • Add your pasta to the water and cook until al dente. As the pasta is cooking, heat the sauce on low. Add 1/4 cup (60ml) of pasta water to loosen the sauce and stir until combined. Add more pasta water as needed. I usually add 1/2 cup (120ml) total before adding the pasta then a splash more once the pasta has been added.
  • Transfer the cooked pasta to the sauce and toss until coated. Add more pasta water as needed until you have a smooth and emulsified sauce.
  • Serve in bowls topped with more Parmigiano.

Notes

  • Prep in advance: the sauce can be prepped in advance and stored in the fridge for up to 2-3 days. Reheat on the stove and loosen with pasta water as directed.
  • I don’t recommend freezing.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is transforming the automotive industry at an unprecedented pace. From manufacturing and vehicle design to maintenance and customer service, AI is helping businesses improve efficiency, reduce costs, and deliver better experiences. As connected vehicles and electric mobility continue to gain momentum, AI is becoming a key driver of innovation across the automotive value chain. One area benefiting significantly from this technological shift is the India Automotive Aftermarket Market, where intelligent technologies are reshaping vehicle servicing, spare parts management, and repair operations.

AI is Reshaping Automotive Manufacturing

Automotive manufacturers are increasingly relying on AI to streamline production and improve quality. Modern factories generate massive amounts of operational data, and AI helps analyze this information to identify bottlenecks, optimize workflows, and reduce waste.

Computer vision systems inspect vehicle components with greater precision than manual methods, while AI-powered robots perform repetitive assembly tasks with speed and consistency. Predictive analytics also allows manufacturers to monitor equipment performance and schedule maintenance before machinery fails, reducing downtime and increasing productivity.

These innovations enable manufacturers to produce safer, more reliable vehicles while lowering operating costs.

Predictive Maintenance Is Changing Vehicle Servicing

One of AI’s most valuable applications in the automotive industry is predictive maintenance. Instead of relying on fixed service schedules, AI continuously analyzes data collected from sensors installed throughout the vehicle.

This technology can monitor:

  • Engine performance
  • Brake condition
  • Battery health
  • Tire pressure
  • Cooling systems
  • Transmission efficiency

When abnormal patterns are detected, the system alerts vehicle owners or service providers before a major failure occurs. Early intervention reduces repair costs, prevents unexpected breakdowns, and extends the lifespan of critical components.

For commercial fleet operators, predictive maintenance also minimizes downtime and improves operational efficiency.

Smarter Diagnostics Improve Repair Accuracy

Vehicle diagnostics have evolved significantly with the introduction of AI. Traditional troubleshooting often depends on manual inspections and technician experience, but AI-powered diagnostic tools can analyze thousands of data points within seconds.

These systems identify faults more accurately and recommend appropriate repair procedures. They can detect problems involving electrical systems, engines, transmissions, batteries, and onboard sensors before symptoms become severe.

As a result, workshops can complete repairs faster while improving service quality and customer satisfaction.

AI Is Accelerating Electric Vehicle Growth

Electric vehicles require advanced monitoring systems to ensure optimal performance and safety. AI plays a critical role in managing battery performance, thermal conditions, charging cycles, and energy consumption.

Modern Battery Management Systems (BMS) use machine learning algorithms to continuously evaluate battery health and optimize charging behaviour. This improves driving range, extends battery lifespan, and reduces the likelihood of unexpected failures.

AI also supports research into new battery materials, allowing manufacturers to develop higher-capacity batteries with faster charging capabilities and improved durability.

As electric mobility expands, service centres are adapting by investing in specialized diagnostic equipment and technician training to support next-generation vehicles.

AI Is Making Supply Chains More Efficient

Managing automotive components and spare parts is becoming increasingly complex as vehicle technologies evolve. AI helps manufacturers, distributors, and service providers forecast demand more accurately by analyzing historical sales, regional trends, seasonal demand, and vehicle usage patterns.

Smarter inventory management offers several benefits:

  • Reduced stock shortages
  • Lower inventory costs
  • Faster order fulfillment
  • Improved warehouse efficiency
  • Better supply chain visibility

These improvements ensure that essential parts are available when needed while minimizing excess inventory.

Transforming Customer Experience

Customer expectations are changing rapidly, and AI is helping automotive businesses provide more personalized and convenient services.

Many companies now use AI-powered platforms to offer:

  • Online service scheduling
  • Digital maintenance records
  • Automated service reminders
  • Virtual customer support
  • Personalized maintenance recommendations
  • Real-time repair updates

These digital services reduce waiting times, improve communication, and create a smoother ownership experience.

AI Is Supporting Smarter Fleet Management

Fleet operators manage hundreds or even thousands of vehicles, making efficient maintenance essential for profitability. AI enables fleet managers to monitor vehicle performance continuously and identify opportunities to improve efficiency.

AI-powered fleet management systems analyze:

  • Fuel consumption
  • Driver behavior
  • Vehicle health
  • Route efficiency
  • Maintenance requirements

These insights help businesses reduce operating expenses, improve safety, and maximize vehicle availability.

Sustainability Through Artificial Intelligence

Sustainability has become a major focus across the automotive industry. AI contributes by helping manufacturers optimize production processes, reduce material waste, and improve energy efficiency.

AI also supports recycling initiatives by identifying reusable materials from vehicle components and improving the sorting of recyclable parts. In addition, intelligent manufacturing systems minimize energy consumption while maintaining consistent product quality.

As companies work toward lower carbon emissions, AI will continue to play an important role in building a more sustainable automotive ecosystem.

Challenges to AI Adoption

Although AI offers significant benefits, its implementation is not without challenges.

High Initial Investment

Advanced AI software, connected equipment, and automation technologies require substantial investment, particularly for smaller businesses.

Workforce Development

The industry needs skilled professionals who understand artificial intelligence, data analytics, robotics, and modern vehicle technologies. Continuous training is essential as vehicles become increasingly software-driven.

Cybersecurity

Connected vehicles and digital manufacturing systems generate vast amounts of data. Protecting this information from cyber threats is becoming a top priority for automotive companies.

Technology Integration

Many organizations still operate legacy systems that are difficult to integrate with modern AI platforms. Successful digital transformation requires careful planning and long-term investment.

The Future of AI in the Automotive Industry

Artificial Intelligence will continue to shape the future of mobility over the coming decade. Emerging technologies such as digital twins, edge computing, autonomous systems, and generative AI will further improve manufacturing efficiency, vehicle safety, and maintenance capabilities.

Connected vehicles will generate even larger volumes of real-time data, enabling more accurate diagnostics and predictive maintenance. Workshops will increasingly rely on AI-powered tools to identify faults, recommend repairs, and manage inventory more efficiently.

At the same time, electric vehicles will drive demand for specialized servicing, battery monitoring, and software-based maintenance solutions. Companies that embrace AI early will be better positioned to adapt to evolving customer expectations and technological advancements.

Conclusion

Artificial Intelligence is no longer an emerging technology—it has become a fundamental part of the automotive industry’s transformation. From intelligent manufacturing and predictive maintenance to advanced diagnostics and smarter supply chains, AI is improving efficiency across every stage of the vehicle lifecycle.

As the industry continues its transition toward connected and electric mobility, businesses that invest in AI-driven innovation will gain a competitive advantage through better operational performance, improved customer experiences, and more sustainable practices. The combination of intelligent technologies, digital infrastructure, and data-driven decision-making will define the next generation of automotive services, creating a more efficient, reliable, and future-ready mobility ecosystem.

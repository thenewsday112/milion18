Extra crunchy chicken cutlets that are topped with a refreshing and crisp brussels sprouts slaw. Tastes amazing, comes together fast and really can be quite versatile!

Years ago, when I first had a crispy chicken cutlet topped with a salad, my mind was blown. I loved the idea. The plating was simple and so was the dish, but it was just so good. Don’t ask me why taking a bite of chicken with the salad on top, as opposed to taking a forkful of chicken and salad together, is so much better.

It’s more fun to eat. And it looks pretty! It’s cute and compact and makes for a fun little dinner.

It’s alllllll the experience over here, if you can’t tell. As a bored millennial who is always looking for the next best thing, a slight change to our favorite meals can really elevate things. 

Because here’s the thing!

This is really just a little cop out. You know I make my chicken cutlets all the time: paper thin, super crispy, requested every single week by my whole family.

But!

I also often make this brussels slaw in the fall and winter. I’ll prep it for my own lunches or even just have it as a side dish to grab really quick for dinner. It’s so ridiculously crunchy and flavorful. If you are a texture nut like I am, this slaw is going to be your new BFF. 

I’m essentially always making these two things, sometimes even in meal form. But am I topping the chicken with the slaw? Never. 

Why not!!!

The slaw is just wonderful.

États-Unis : les coulisses du métro de New York vues par Christopher Payne

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

La Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA) a annoncé le 22 septembre dernier l’ouverture de « In Service: MTA Photographs by Christopher Payne », l’exposition d’une série de 50 photographies qui offre aux usagers un regard rare sur les ateliers de maintenance et les installations industrielles qui font fonctionner le réseau.

Christopher Payne, photographe spécialisé dans les sujets industriels et architecturaux, a visité treize installations et usines réparties dans quatre États. Il s’est également concentré sur les travaux d’infrastructure invisibles du grand public et sur les savoir-faire spécialisés nécessaires pour construire et entretenir les machines.

Le projet s’inspire de la série MTA Overhaul Shops, réalisée par Christopher Payne dans deux ateliers de maintenance, et publiée dans un article du New York Times en 2024. Dix photographies de cette série servent de socle à l’exposition. Les 40 autres images ont été commandées par MTA Arts & Design et réalisées entre mai et août 2026.

Le photographe a documenté le travail quotidien de maintenance dans chacune des divisions de l’agence. Il a également parcouru le pays pour photographier la construction de matériel roulant. Il s’est notamment rendu sur les sites d’assemblage des rames de métro R211 de Kawasaki, à Yonkers (New York) et à Lincoln (Nebraska), sur les sites de production de bus articulés de New Flyer, à St. Cloud (Minnesota) et à Jamestown (New York), ainsi que dans l’usine de locomotives de Siemens à Sacramento (Californie).

Depuis près de quarante ans, MTA Arts & Design transforme d’anciens caissons publicitaires désaffectés en galeries d’art. Cette fois, les images de la série In Service occuperont tous les caissons lumineux de cinq grandes stations (42 St-Bryant Park, Atlantic Av-Barclays Ctr, Bowling Green, Grand Central Terminal, Dining Concourse et Grand Central Madison) pendant six mois.

I'm going to be a bit unusual with the Introduction this week because it's been an unusual week – the sports pages broke a botanical story about a newly discovered plant species that is endemic to the Andes of Peru.

Researchers identified a new species of Salvia, and because the flower's colouring is similar to the Argentinean flag, they decided to honour Lionel Messi, widely regarded as Argentina's best football player of all time, who retired from international competition after this year's World Cup, by naming the species after him.

Sports enthusiasts learned that the new species will be called Salvia messii. And the announcement is so recent, I was unable to find a verified picture using the species name. So the unusual bit of the Introduction is that I'm including the lead researcher's Instagram announcement. The video is in Spanish, but even if you don't speak Spanish, the images speak for themselves.

View this post on Instagram

Amazingly, this means Messi has broken his own Guinness World Record for most species named after a football player. His other eponymous species are a lizard, spider, Miocene-era fossil brachiopod, Pliocene-era fossil condor, and a fungus. Sadly, Salvia messii is already considered endangered and in need of conservation. But hopefully its association with the sports world will bring needed attention to the species, its habitat, and conservation efforts generally to a wider, nonacademic audience.

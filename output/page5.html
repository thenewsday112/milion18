Which 'Criminal Minds' Stars Are — And Aren't — Returning After Cast Exit?

📅 Nov 02, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Criminal Minds is wrapped up with season 20 — but not every cast member is coming back after a shocking exit.

Criminal Minds, which premiered in 2005, follows a group of criminal profilers who work for the FBI as members of its Behavioral Analysis Unit. The crime drama explores different fictional cases and shows how behavioral analysis helps the team locate their unknown subjects.

The original Criminal Minds series was revived by Paramount+ after its initial conclusion in 2020. Criminal Minds: Evolution brought back most of the cast with Joe Mantegna, A.J. Cook, Vangsness, Aisha Tyler, Adam Rodriguez and Paget Brewster reprising their roles. Other key characters — such as Matthew Gray Gubler‘s Reid — have made cameos.

The first season of Criminal Minds: Evolution (a.k.a. season 16 of the OG Criminal Minds series) introduced Voit as the main antagonist. The BAU spent the entire season hunting down the prolific killer, who they linked to 62 murders and was the mastermind behind setting up an online network of fellow serial killers.

‘Criminal Minds’ Cast: Then and Now

Voit’s status as the villain was left up in the air when he had significant memory loss that caused him to no longer be labeled as a psychopath. Gilford and the rest of the Criminal Minds cast have acknowledged that Voit can’t remain on the show for good.

“I was supposed to leave the show a while ago and I’ve been pitching, ‘Well, I have to leave at some point. If you’re not going to kill me, we should have a spinoff,'” Gilford quipped exclusively to Us in May 2025. “It’s called Criminal Mind: Voit and it follows what Voit is doing when he is out in the world. Somehow he either escapes or he gets released and he is this white whale to the BAU. So Criminal Minds can keep going and having their [unsub] of the week — but they’ll always be like, ‘I wonder if Voit had something to do with this.'”

Voit was ultimately written off ahead of season 20.

“We can both hope [my character Voit will return] but all I know is I’m not there filming right now,” he told Us in July 2026 of his potential return for season 20 of Criminal Minds: Evolution. “It was hard to say goodbye. It definitely was one of my favorite characters I’ve ever gotten to play and partially because what a gift.”

He continued: “Every season I got to do a different side of this character. It wasn’t like I was just doing the same thing every year.”

For Gilford, Criminal Minds was the perfect challenge, adding, “To give this guy some redemption and go out on a good guy note — so to speak — was nice. It’s weird. You wrap a show and a lot of times you’re like, ‘I don’t know if I’m coming back next year and if we’re going to have another season in general.’ On this, I did know that it was the end.”

Gilford told Us he struggled to say goodbye to Criminal Minds.

“It was a bummer and I am really like, ‘I’m not good with emotions,'” he shared. “So I literally — before they even officially wrapped — I had a feeling it was the last take. I just left. I didn’t say goodbye to anyone. I just snuck out. They’re like, ‘Zach, where are you? Where’d you go?’ And then a few people saw me driving out of my car. I’m like, ‘Bye, I’ll see you later.'”

Clayface Comes in Second to You Can See Everything at the Domestic Box Office on Halloween Weekend

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Clayface has come in second at the domestic box office on the Halloween weekend as audiences continue to show up for Elizabeth Holmes documentary You Can See Everything.

It was a narrow victory for Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim's comedic look into the life of the controversial Theranos creator, thanks to an estimated haul of $10.6 million to the DCU movie's $10.4 million (Note: These positions could change once final totals are tallied Monday). You Can See Everything's limited international rollout means Clayface is comfortably first globally ($22.8 million), but in what is technically the doc's third weekend in theaters, becoming the number one movie in the domestic market is still an impressive feat.

It's less of a warning of how audiences feel about Clayface and more of a sign that the behind-the-scenes peek at Holmes' life has real staying power. Originally released to just 855 theaters October 16, the Fielder doc has benefited from strong word-of-mouth and stellar reviews (IGN gave it a 10/10). Movie social media platform Letterboxd even pointed out that the project quickly emerged as its highest-rated feature film ever, with its first weekend in theaters landing it in second behind Street Fighter.

Returning with more auditoriums under its belt, You Can See Everything came in second again when Clayface brought moviegoers to a new Gotham City for its debut last weekend. With the spooky holiday bolstering the atmosphere, many wondered if DC's body horror movie could fend off the competition for Halloween, but at this point, at least, Holmes' story persevered.

Really, though, both films performed well over the past three days. Domestically, after a box office increase in its second weekend, You Can See Everything dropped just 43%, while Clayface returned for its second bout with a drop of 57%. Both have relatively modest budgets, too, with the former packing a reported budget of less than $20 million when factoring in both production and marketing costs, while the latter is believed to have a $44 million production budget. You Can See Everything has accumulated $64 million thus far, with Clayface's global total standing at $82.9 million.

Clayface brings an often overlooked Batman villain to the big screen with a tragic backstory starring Tom Rhys Harries and Naomi Ackie. It also marked an important moment for DC Studios' DCU, which suffered some setbacks after Supergirl failed to successfully follow up on 2025's Superman when it premiered earlier this year. Even if it's not a home run, the villain origin movie seems to have done its job so far, with the weeks ahead sure to keep increasing its total theatrical haul.

Meanwhile, at the worldwide box office, Resident Evil jumped from coming in fifth last weekend to being fourth for Halloween. The Zach Cregger video game adaptation has now reached $400 million against its reported $75 million budget. There's also Laika's critically acclaimed Wildwood, which is still trying to make a dent in its reported $100 million budget. Its worldwide pull for the weekend was $11.54 million, bringing its total after two weekends to $35.17 million.

Some of October's biggest releases arrived earlier in the month, but moviegoers have an even spread of headliners to look forward to in the weeks ahead. Director Takashi Yamazaki's kaiju sequel, Godzilla Minus Zero, stomps into theaters November 6 (or earlier if you're looking to catch a Godzilla Day showing November 3). I Play Rocky will offer a new perspective on a classic sports drama this weekend, too, with The Cat in the Hat also set to release then.

Denis Villeneuve's first two Dune movies get the re-release treatment November 13 as viewers prep for Part 3. Then, come November 20, there's the latest Hunger Games prequel film, Sunrise on the Reaping. Focker In-Law stars Ben Stiller, Ariana Grande, Robert De Niro, and Skyler Gisondo November 25.

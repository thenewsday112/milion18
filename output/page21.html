New community fundraising initiative launches at 18 restaurants, connecting neighborhood schools, nonprofits and local organizations with opportunities to raise funds while bringing people together over great food

Dallas, TX  (RestaurantNews.com)  Rusty Taco the fast-casual restaurant brand known for its fresh, street-style tacos, welcoming atmosphere and neighborhood roots, is strengthening its commitment to community engagement through a new partnership with Force4Good, a technology platform designed to make local fundraising easier, more accessible and more effective for restaurants and the organizations they support.

Launched in October 2026 across 18 participating Rusty Taco restaurants, the initiative creates new opportunities for local schools, youth sports teams, nonprofits and community organizations to connect with Rusty Taco franchisees and host fundraising events that bring people together for a shared purpose.

The partnership represents an important step in Rusty Taco’s ongoing commitment to supporting the neighborhoods it serves, while giving franchise operators additional resources to establish meaningful relationships with local organizations.

“Rusty Taco has always been about more than great tacos. It’s about creating a place where people feel welcome, where neighbors connect and where communities come together,” said Michael Meche, President of Rusty Taco. “Our partnership with Force4Good gives our franchisees a simple, sustainable way to turn those values into action. Whether it’s helping a local school raise funds, supporting a youth sports team or partnering with a neighborhood nonprofit, we want Rusty Taco to be a place that makes a positive difference.”

Making Community Fundraising Easier and More Accessible

Through Force4Good, participating Rusty Taco restaurants can streamline the entire fundraising process, from coordinating events and promoting them to tracking sales and managing fundraising administration.

The platform helps eliminate many of the time-consuming logistical challenges traditionally associated with restaurant fundraising events, making it easier for franchisees to support local causes while remaining focused on delivering a great guest experience. For participating organizations, the initiative offers an accessible way to connect with a neighborhood restaurant, engage supporters and raise funds through community dining events.

The partnership is designed to provide several benefits:

  • For local schools and nonprofits: Opportunities to organize restaurant-based fundraising events that bring together families, supporters and community members.
  • For Rusty Taco franchisees: A repeatable local marketing and community engagement program that simplifies planning, promotion and reporting.
  • For restaurant guests: New opportunities to support causes that matter to them while enjoying their favorite Rusty Taco menu items.
  • For local communities: A collaborative approach to strengthening relationships between neighborhood businesses and the organizations working to make a difference.

By simplifying the fundraising experience, Rusty Taco hopes to encourage more frequent and meaningful collaboration between participating restaurants and the communities they serve.

A Proven Platform for Community Engagement

Force4Good works with thousands of restaurant and retail locations to make fundraising and community partnerships easier to organize and measure. According to Force4Good’s published platform statistics, more than 9,500 retail locations use its community giving technology, while more than 45,000 nonprofit organizations are part of its fundraising network.

Force4Good also reports that its marketing programs helped secure more than 2,250 fundraising events in 2025, generating approximately $1.77 million in restaurant sales across participating businesses. These platform-wide results demonstrate the opportunity for restaurants to combine community support with customer engagement and sustainable local marketing.

For Rusty Taco, the partnership provides the infrastructure to develop those opportunities across its franchise network, creating a more consistent approach to community involvement while preserving each restaurant’s ability to build relationships with organizations in its own neighborhood.

Supporting Franchisees and Building Stronger Neighborhood Connections

As Rusty Taco continues to grow, maintaining its identity as a neighborhood gathering place remains an important part of the brand’s strategy. The Force4Good partnership reflects the belief that restaurants can play a meaningful role in their communities beyond serving food. By providing franchisees with the tools to coordinate fundraising events, promote local partnerships and track participation, Rusty Taco is creating a scalable program that supports both community involvement and restaurant-level business development.

“Community engagement shouldn’t be complicated or limited to a once-a-year event,” added Meche. “We want to make it easier for our franchisees to say yes when a local organization reaches out and to build relationships that continue long after a single fundraiser.”

The program also offers an opportunity to introduce Rusty Taco to new guests, encourage repeat visits and create shared experiences centered around food and community.

Looking Ahead to 2027

Rusty Taco launched the Force4Good partnership in October 2026 with 18 participating restaurants and plans to add three additional locations during the first quarter of 2027. As the initiative develops, participating restaurants will have opportunities to establish partnerships with local schools, sports organizations, charitable groups and other neighborhood organizations.

The company anticipates that the program will become an increasingly valuable part of its community engagement strategy, helping franchisees build stronger local relationships while creating new opportunities for organizations to raise awareness and support for their missions.

For Rusty Taco, the goal is simple: make it easier for neighborhoods to come together, support one another and enjoy great food along the way. For more information, visit RustyTaco.com.

About Force4Good

Force4Good is a community fundraising and giving platform that helps restaurants, retailers and nonprofit organizations simplify the process of organizing fundraising events, coordinating donations and sponsorships, promoting local partnerships and tracking results. Through its technology and marketing tools, Force4Good helps businesses turn community engagement into repeatable, measurable programs. For more information, visit Force4Good.com.

About Rusty Taco

Founded in Dallas in 2010, Rusty Taco is a fast-casual neighborhood taco stand serving authentic street-style tacos made with fresh ingredients, bold flavors and made-to-order preparation. Inspired by founder Rusty Fenton’s vision of bringing people together over simple, high-quality food, the brand has grown to 28 locations across multiple states while remaining committed to genuine hospitality and community connection. In addition to its signature tacos, Rusty Taco offers breakfast tacos, burritos, bowls, quesadillas, handcrafted margaritas, local beer and catering for gatherings of all sizes. By combining craveable food, an approachable atmosphere and a passion for bringing neighbors together, Rusty Taco has become a favorite destination for breakfast, lunch, dinner and celebrations. Learn more at RustyTaco.com.

The Mongoose Review

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Mongoose is in theaters now.

There’s a scene in the 1971 sci-fi cult classic The Omega Man where Charlton Heston’s helicopter is crash-landing, and just before the moment of impact it flies into an embankment just below the camera’s POV… where the craft’s explosion happens safely off-screen. It was a cheapo move in 1971 – they clearly didn’t really blow up the helicopter – and it’s a cheapo move in 2026, too, when Liam Neeson’s The Mongoose – now, somehow, playing at a multiplex near you! – pulls a similar stunt.

Sure, in The Mongoose the helicopter doesn’t even blow up off-camera, but that’s not the point (the crash-landing winds up being played for a laugh instead). What I’m talking about here is the threadbare sense that such a moment evokes for the viewer, the “a third of the budget of this movie must’ve gone to Liam Neeson” feeling that permeates the not-quite high-octane, not-quite thrilling picture.

And yet, there’s a likability factor wafting around this story, thanks largely to Neeson’s disarming “yeah, I am taking a third of this film’s budget, and I’m gonna have fun doing it” affectation as he drives a stolen hotrod – do we still call them hotrods? – across various states while on the run from the police due to a crime he did not commit. Along for the ride, so to speak, are Ving Rhames, Marisa Tomei, and Michael Chiklis, all cashing in less than a third of the budget, presumably, but hitting their marks efficiently enough to get this thing over the finish line in a “Maybe just catch it on Prime Video eventually” kind of way.

The Mongoose tells its story through a mix of the chase Neeson’s Ryan “Fang” Flanagan is involved in and flashbacks to the former Special Forces soldier’s time in Afghanistan, specifically when he and his comrades (Rhames’ Tanker, Marcus Thomas’ Munch, and Ryan Morgan's Wiz) were captured by the enemy. Further flashbacks eventually fill in why Fang is on the run; specifically, his ex-wife Tara’s (Marisa Tomei) fiancé – “Todd something or other,” Fang growls – turns out to be not a nice person, and when Fang attempts to help Tara move out of the guy’s place, violence erupts. Next thing he knows, someone’s dead, and he’s stolen Todd something or other’s fancy, and quite fast, Ford Mustang. Other than that, all he’s got is a bullet in his leg, a vow to never be imprisoned again after his experience in the war, and the Mustang's built-in AI assistant, named Helene. “Is this, like, Siri?” Fang – and maybe Neeson too when he first read the script – asks.

Neeson's Fang is surprisingly chill about this situation, as he clearly has a death wish, having decided that he is not going to allow himself to be taken alive.

Helene, like many of the other characters (“characters”) in the episodic The Mongoose, serves as a foil for the surprisingly at-ease Fang to toss one-liners at while out-driving the various police who are pursuing him by land and air. Fang is surprisingly chill about this situation, as he clearly has a death wish, having decided that he is not going to allow himself to be taken alive. As the chase drags on, he eventually finds temporary refuge through the help of some other vets, and soon Fang begins to take on a legendary man-against-the-system status, thanks no doubt to his war hero cred, as well as the “live” CNN coverage of the high-speed pursuit, which IRL TV personality Laura Coates awkwardly hosts.

On the side of the law, there’s Maurice Marvel Meredith as Major James Tilton and Chiklis as Major Walter Pope. Suffice to say, one’s the good cop and one’s the bad copy, and never the twain shall meet, but also, never the twain shall have anything interesting or realistic to do or say. Rhames, unfortunately, seems to be struggling to deliver his lines at times, even while his very presence brings a sense of good will to the film. Tomei, meanwhile, is always likable, but most of her scenes are essentially just her on the phone with Neeson’s character, even as she miraculously overcomes some seriously life-changing events over the course of 24 hours or so.

As for the Mongoose of the title, it’s the beach in Mexico where Fang and Tara had their best days together, and as such it’s the ideal that Fang has fixated on – his “retirement” – if he somehow can escape his current, dire situation. But what he also must escape is the guilt of the men he left behind in Afghanistan, and it is this thread that director Mark Vanselow somehow scores with by the time the credits roll, aided no doubt by some keenly picked, and timed, needledrops, and a couple of very cleverly edited shots in the final moments of the film. The Mongoose may be working just one level above a direct-to-video cheapie for a lot of its running time, but it’s still got some heart, too.

America  (RestaurantNews.com)  On Veterans Day, America’s heroes will have opportunities to enjoy complimentary meals at restaurants across the country. Shoney’s, which for 80 years has served as America’s Dinner Table and a welcome home sign to veterans and troops, has a special invitation: START YOUR DAY AT SHONEY’S.

On Wednesday, November 11, from opening until 11 a.m., Shoney’s will honor all U.S. veterans and active-duty military personnel with a complimentary, all-you-care-to-eat Breakfast Fresh Food Bar at participating restaurants. It’s a gesture of gratitude from a brand that has long considered America’s military part of its extended family. For Shoney’s Chairman and CEO, Mr. David Davoudpour, it’s personal.

David Davoudpour, Shoney’s Chairman and CEO

“Veterans Day is going to be a great day across America, with restaurants and businesses coming together to honor our veterans and troops, all heroes in our eyes,” said Mr. Davoudpour. “There will be plenty of opportunities for our heroes to enjoy a free meal throughout the day, and we applaud every business that participates. At Shoney’s, we want to start their day by serving them what many consider the most important meal of the day: breakfast.”

Shoney’s celebrated Breakfast Fresh Food Bar, a cornerstone of the brand’s all-American dining tradition, offers guests an abundance of freshly prepared breakfast favorites, including scrambled eggs, bacon, sausage, biscuits and gravy, pancakes and more, with selections varying by location.

David Davoudpour,
Shoney’s Chairman and CEO

“We salute you, we applaud you, and most importantly, we thank you,” continued Mr. Davoudpour. “For 80 years, Shoney’s has welcomed generations of American families to our tables, including the brave men and women who have served our country. We want every veteran and active-duty military guest who walks through our doors on November 11 to know how much their service and sacrifice mean to us. Our breakfast is a small gesture compared to what they’ve given our nation, but it comes with enormous gratitude.”

AMERICA’S DINNER TABLE | A WELCOME HOME SIGN FOR 80 YEARS

Shoney’s relationship with America’s military extends well beyond one day on the calendar. The iconic restaurant brand has long embraced Veterans Day as an opportunity to personally recognize those who have defended the nation’s freedoms, welcoming military guests with the same Southern hospitality that has defined the brand for eight decades.

For Mr. Davoudpour, that commitment also extends beyond the restaurant. Earlier this year, he personally contributed $100,000 to the Lumpkin County Veterans Affairs Committee in Dahlonega, Georgia, supporting the development of a local veterans cemetery designed to provide a lasting place of honor for those who served.

“American businesses and our military share an extraordinary bond,” Mr. Davoudpour added. “We enjoy the freedoms and opportunities made possible by those willing to serve and sacrifice. Veterans Day gives all of us an opportunity to say thank you, and at Shoney’s, we intend to make that thank you meaningful.”

The complimentary Breakfast Fresh Food Bar will be available to veterans and active-duty military personnel from opening until 11 a.m. on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, at participating Shoney’s restaurants. Copycat Taco Bell Caramel Apple Empanadas

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 1-1/2 cups finely chopped peeled tart apples
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/8 teaspoon ground allspice
  • 1/3 cup thick caramel apple dip
  • 2 sheets refrigerated pie crust
  • 1 large egg yolk
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • 1/4 cup butter, melted
  • 1 cup cinnamon sugar
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. In a small saucepan, combine apples, sugar, cinnamon and allspice. Cook until apples are tender, 4-5 minutes. Drain; discard liquid. Transfer to a small bowl. Add caramel; stir to coat. Scoop by tablespoonfuls onto waxed paper. Freeze until firm, about 1 hour.
  2. Unroll pie crust; cut into 14 circles using a 4-in. circle cookie cutter (rerolling dough scraps as needed).
  3. In a small bowl, whisk together egg yolk and water. Brush edges of crusts with egg wash; place apple filling in the center. Fold crusts over filling and seal edges well with a fork. Place 2 in. apart on parchment-lined baking sheets. Freeze until firm, about 30 minutes.
  4. Brush remaining egg wash over tops. Cut 2 small slits on the top of each empanada.
  5. Bake at 425° for 12-14 minutes or until golden brown. Remove from oven and brush with melted butter. Carefully toss in cinnamon sugar. Serve warm.

Nutrition Facts

1 piece: 234 calories, 13g fat (6g saturated fat), 29mg cholesterol, 159mg sodium, 28g carbohydrate (14g sugars, 0 fiber), 2g protein.

Cherry Peach Pie with Salted Pecan Streusel

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ingredients

  • 1-1/3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1-1/2 teaspoons sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon pumpkin pie spice
  • 4 tablespoons shortening, cubed
  • 4 tablespoons cold butter, cubed
  • 3 to 4 tablespoons water
  • TOPPING:
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 1/4 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/4 cup crushed gingersnap cookies
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup old-fashioned oats
  • 1/2 cup chopped pecans
  • 1/4 cup butter, melted
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • FILLING:
  • 2 cups sliced peeled peaches
  • 1 cup frozen pitted dark sweet cherries, thawed
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/3 cup sugar
  • 1/3 cup packed brown sugar
  • 4 tablespoons potato starch
  • Pinch salt
Text Ingredients

Directions

  1. In a large bowl, mix flour, sugar, salt and pie spice; cut in shortening and butter until crumbly. Gradually add ice water, tossing with a fork until dough holds together when pressed. Shape into a disk; wrap and refrigerate 1 hour or overnight.
  2. Preheat oven to 400°. On a lightly floured surface, roll dough to a 1/8-in.-thick circle; transfer to a 9-in. pie plate. Trim crust to 1/2 in. beyond rim of plate; flute edge. Refrigerate while preparing filling.
  3. For the topping, in a medium bowl, whisk together flour, sugars, crushed cookies and salt. Stir in oats and pecans. Add the butter and vanilla until small clumps form. Set aside.
  4. In a large bowl, toss peaches and cherries with lemon juice and vanilla. In a separate bowl, whisk together sugars, potato starch and salt. Add to fruit, toss until well coated.
  5. Transfer filling to prepared crust. Sprinkle with reserved topping; pressing down to adhere as needed. Bake for 20 minutes. Reduce oven setting to 350°, bake until crust and topping are golden brown and fruit is bubbly, 20-25 minutes longer. Cool completely on a wire rack.

Nutrition Facts

1 piece: 503 calories, 23g fat (9g saturated fat), 31mg cholesterol, 563mg sodium, 70g carbohydrate (38g sugars, 2g fiber), 5g protein.
